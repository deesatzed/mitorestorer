\begin{abstract}
Restoring bioenergetic function in cells burdened by mutant or damaged mitochondrial populations ($D$) alongside wild-type populations ($W$) requires navigating nonlinear couplings among autophagic clearance bottlenecks ($B$), reactive oxygen species (ROS) conversion, and replicative selection ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} \ge 1$). Here we formulate an eight-state ordinary differential equation (ODE) model with exact 1-to-1 correspondence between analytical equations, IEEE-754 \texttt{FP64} simulation, and bare-metal C99 firmware, analyzing a three-input staged feedback policy: Stage~0 bottleneck clearance ($u_0$), Stage~1 selective mitophagy ($u_1$), and Stage~2 biogenesis ($u_2$) gated jointly at $h(t) = D(t)/(W(t)+D(t)) \le h_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$ and $B(t) \le B_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.120$. Using Nagumo's subtangent condition, we prove joint forward invariance of the Stage-1 safe set ($\mathrm{EP} \le 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$, $W_{\min} = 0.600 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$, $\lambda_{\min} = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1}$) and derive both an exact accumulated-selection identity $s(t) = \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq$ (supremum error $0.0000$, $s^*(0.40, 0.08) = 2.0369$) and a zero-tuning closed-form perturbation bound for constant-rate rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$ across three regimes with distinct dimensionless $\Pi$-groups ($R^2 = 0.9828$, supremum error $0.0493 \le 0.1216$). Solving the 300-variable discrete-adjoint optimal control problem (\texttt{L-BFGS-B}, gradient error $1.95 \times 10^{-9}$, 5-start spread $0.12\%$) shows that Pontryagin-optimal trajectories ride an interior mitophagy arc $u_1^*(t) \in (0, 1)$ along $\mathrm{EP} = \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ before a terminal biogenesis sprint ($J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$ vs.\ two-stage relay $J_{\mathrm{2stage}}^* = 0.850$ and threshold feedback $J_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 0.8875$), trading $+22.21\%$ open-loop Bolza cost for $20.1\%$ redox margin. Mapping the $31 \times 31$ $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ phase plane delineates irreversible collapse ($18.9\%$) and reconciles build-first rescue ($2.9\%$) with purge-then-build control. Under four-channel proxy sensing ($\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$), $\pm 20\%$ 7-rate mismatch, $\pm 8\%$ initial-state uncertainty, $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ noise, and $5\%$ $6.5\sigma$ spikes, margin-tightened Huber-EKF ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.065$) reduces premature switches from $24.17\%$ to $5.00\%$. On host-emulated Google Coral NPU (\texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x}, \texttt{sizeof} static workspace $= 3{,}696\text{ B} / 8{,}192\text{ B}$ in L1 DTCM Bank~0), our C99 fixed-point Huber-EKF ($0.94\%$ $\hat{h}$ RMSE) and \texttt{Q16.16} \texttt{RK4} kernel with \texttt{Q8.24} slopes and $\Sigma\Delta$ remainder diffusion eliminate deadband stalling across a $16\times$ step sweep ($0.0534\%\text{--}0.0563\%$ vs.\ $100\%$ naive \texttt{Q16.16} stall at $N=8{,}000$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Nonlinear Coupling in Mitochondrial Quality Control}
Eukaryotic bioenergetics depends on maintaining a high functional fraction of intact wild-type mitochondrial DNA and cristae ($W$) relative to deletion-bearing or depolarized mutant organelles ($D$) \cite{wallace2012mitochondrial,stewart2015purifying,aryaman2019stochastic}. Across diverse biological timescales---including post-mitotic dopaminergic neurons in Parkinsonian neurodegeneration ($T_{\max} = 90\text{ d}$) \cite{surmeier2017selective,bolam2012living}, \textit{ex vivo} chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell expansion bioreactors ($T_{\max} = 168\text{ h}$) \cite{fraietta2018determinants,sukumar2013inhibiting,buck2016mitochondrial}, and honeybee (\textit{Apis mellifera}) thoracic flight muscle exposed to strobilurin Complex~III inhibition ($T_{\max} = 14\text{ d}$) \cite{degrandi2015fungicides,seehuus2006bee}---mitochondrial decline is governed by three interacting feedbacks:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Upstream Clearance Bottlenecks ($B$):} Accumulation of $\alpha$-synuclein oligomers, lipofuscin, or xenobiotic fungicides elevates energetic impedance and saturates autophagosome-lysosome flux $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B)$ \cite{surmeier2017selective,ryu2016urolithin}.
  \item \textbf{Threshold ROS Conversion ($\mathrm{EP} > \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$):} When cellular energetic demand and impedance exceed the glutathione/peroxiredoxin antioxidant buffering threshold $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$, electron leak generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) that convert intact wild-type mass $W$ into damaged mass $D$.
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric Replicative Selection ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}$):} Deletion-bearing mtDNA templates or fragmented organelles frequently exhibit a replicative or transcription-coupled copy-number advantage ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} \ge 1$) during non-selective biogenesis \cite{stewart2015purifying,kowald2014mtDNA}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with Prior Control Formulations}
In a foundational mathematical study, Hoitzing et al.~\cite{hoitzing2019energetic} analyzed stochastic and deterministic mtDNA population control in a two-species $(W, D)$ system without an upstream lysosomal bottleneck state $B(t)$ or replicative advantage ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} = 1$). When wild-type mass $W_0$ is already near the minimum survival floor and $B_0 \approx 0$, Hoitzing et al.\ demonstrated that an initial biogenesis pulse (``build-first'') prevents transient ATP starvation before selective clearance. Conversely, when an upstream bottleneck $B_0 > 0$ elevates bioenergetic impedance and $k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} > 1$, stimulating biogenesis ($u_2 > 0$) prior to clearing $B$ and $D$ drives the energy potential $\mathrm{EP}(t)$ above $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ and expands the mutant pool. Understanding the precise mathematical boundary between these two regimes---and implementing a verifiable closed-loop controller within the strict memory and arithmetic constraints of an ultra-low-power implantable or bioreactor neural processing unit (NPU)---remains an open systems-and-control problem.

\subsection{Contributions and Scope}
This paper makes four self-contained, computationally verified contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unified 8-State ODE Model and Joint Nagumo Safety Invariance (\S2.1--\S2.2):} We formulate an 8-state ODE plant with closed-form auxiliary rates unified 1-to-1 across LaTeX, Python, and C99, prove joint forward invariance of the Stage-1 safe box via Nagumo's subtangent condition (proving $\mathrm{EP} \le 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ dynamically and $W_{\min} = 0.600 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$, Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}), and derive an analytical uniform contraction lower bound $\lambda_{\min} = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}).
  \item \textbf{Multi-Start Adjoint Optimal Control and 2D Controllability Phase Diagram (\S2.3--\S2.4):} We solve the unconstrained 300-variable discrete-adjoint optimal control problem (\texttt{L-BFGS-B} across 5 diverse initializations, $0.12\%$ cost spread) to decompose the optimality gap between Pontryagin interior-arc modulation ($J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$), optimal two-stage constant relay control ($J_{\mathrm{2stage}}^* = 0.850$), and closed-loop threshold feedback ($J_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 0.8875$), and map the $31 \times 31$ $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ phase diagram delineating irreversible collapse ($18.9\%$) and Hoitzing et al.'s build-first rescue wedge ($2.9\%$).
  \item \textbf{Accumulated-Selection Identity and Zero-Tuning Perturbation Bound (\S3):} We separate the exact accumulated-selection coordinate $s(t) = \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq$ (zero reduction error in the sub-damage regime) from constant-rate rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$ computed via a closed-form Stage-0/1 bottleneck integral across three regimes with distinct dimensionless $\Pi$-groups, verifying analytical error bounds in every regime (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, Table~\ref{tab:collapse}).
  \item \textbf{Mismatched Output-Feedback Huber-EKF and \texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x} Firmware (\S4--\S5):} We evaluate the Stage-1 7-state Observability Gramian ($\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$), benchmark both Python Monte Carlo and bare-metal C99 fixed-point Huber-EKF under $\pm 20\%$ kinetic mismatch and $6.5\sigma$ sensor spikes, prove a reversible Gaussian $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ Markov persistence bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence}) with equal-footing plant-preserving actuator rollback ($\Delta h_{\max} = 0.0098$), and verify single-bank L1 DTCM residency (\texttt{sizeof} $= 3{,}696\text{ B}$) and deadband-free $\Sigma\Delta$ \texttt{RK4} integration ($0.0534\%\text{--}0.0563\%$ error across $N \in [500, 8{,}000]$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Model and Staged Control Analysis}

\subsection{Eight-State Dynamical System and Closed-Form Auxiliary Rates}
We model the intracellular mitochondrial network on a finite horizon $t \in [0, T_{\max}]$ via the state vector
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}(t) = \big[W,\, D,\, B,\, E,\, G,\, N,\, C,\, M_8\big]^\top \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^8,
  \label{eq:state_vector}
\end{equation}
where $W(t)$ is normalized intact wild-type mitochondrial mass (healthy homeostatic baseline $W^* = 1.00$), $D(t)$ is normalized deletion-bearing or depolarized mutant mitochondrial mass, $B(t)$ is the upstream proteostatic/xenobiotic clearance bottleneck load, $E(t)$ is filtered cellular energy-resistance potential, $G(t)$ is a secreted/circulating metabolic stress readout (e.g., GDF15 or extracellular lactate accumulation), $N(t)$ is the normalized $\text{NAD}^+/\text{NADH}$ redox cofactor pool, $C(t)$ is phosphocreatine/shuttle buffer capacity, and $M_8(t)$ is a downstream epigenetic/exhaustion drift readout state.

Let $M(t) = W(t) + D(t)$ denote total mitochondrial mass and $h(t) = D(t)/M(t) \in [0, 1)$ denote the mutant heteroplasmy fraction. The system is actuated by a bounded three-dimensional control vector $\mathbf{u}(t) = [u_0(t), u_1(t), u_2(t)]^\top \in [0, 1]^3$, where $u_0$ drives upstream bottleneck clearance, $u_1$ drives PINK1/Parkin-mediated selective mitophagy of $D$, and $u_2$ drives PGC-1$\alpha$/TFAM-mediated mitochondrial biogenesis.

To guarantee exact 1-to-1 correspondence between the mathematical analysis, the floating-point benchmark suite (\texttt{eval\_rhs\_fp64}), and the bare-metal C99 fixed-point kernel (\texttt{coralnpu\_eval\_core8\_rhs\_q16}), all auxiliary rates are defined in closed form without unmodeled factors:
\begin{align}
  V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) &= \frac{K_B}{K_B + B}, \label{eq:aux_vlys} \\
  k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N) &= k_{\mathrm{bio},0} + (k_{\mathrm{bio},\max} - k_{\mathrm{bio},0})\,u_2\,\frac{N}{1 + N}, \label{eq:aux_kbio} \\
  k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1) &= k_{\mathrm{mito},W} + (k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W})\,u_1, \label{eq:aux_kmitod} \\
  \mathrm{ATP}(\mathbf{x}) &= (W + \eta_D D)(1 + \gamma_C C), \label{eq:aux_atp} \\
  \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1 + \beta_B B + \beta_h h}{\max(0.10,\, W + \eta_D D)(1 + \gamma_C C)}, \label{eq:aux_er} \\
  f_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_1, u_2) &= 1 - c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2, \label{eq:aux_zeff} \\
  \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) &= \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x})\,f_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_1, u_2)^2. \label{eq:aux_ep}
\end{align}
Here $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) \in (0, 1]$ is the Michaelis-Menten lysosomal competence factor with half-saturation $K_B > 0$; $k_{\mathrm{bio},0}$ is the unstimulated basal biogenesis rate calibrated so that $(W^*, D^*) = (1.00, 0)$ is the exact basal equilibrium under $k_{\mathrm{mito},W}$:
\begin{equation}
  k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\left(1 - \frac{W^*}{K}\right) = k_{\mathrm{mito},W} \implies k_{\mathrm{bio},0} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},W}}{1 - 1/K}.
  \label{eq:kbio0_calibration}
\end{equation}
For $K = 1.250$ and $k_{\mathrm{mito},W} = 0.015\text{ d}^{-1}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:kbio0_calibration} yields the exact basal turnover rate $k_{\mathrm{bio},0} = 0.075\text{ d}^{-1}$.

The eight-state ODE system is given by:
\begin{align}
  \dot{W} &= k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\,W\left(1 - \frac{W+D}{K}\right) \nonumber\\
          &\quad - \Big(k_{\mathrm{mito},W} + k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)\Big)W, \label{eq:ode_W} \\
  \dot{D} &= k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\,D\left(1 - \frac{W+D}{K}\right) \nonumber\\
          &\quad - k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B)\,D \nonumber\\
          &\quad + k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)W, \label{eq:ode_D} \\
  \dot{B} &= b_{\mathrm{basal}} + k_{\mathrm{acc}}\,D\,(1 - u_0) - k_{\mathrm{clr}}\,u_0\,B, \label{eq:ode_B} \\
  \dot{E} &= k_E\big(\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - E\big), \label{eq:ode_E} \\
  \dot{G} &= k_{G,\mathrm{prod}}\,E - k_{G,\mathrm{clr}}\,G, \label{eq:ode_G} \\
  \dot{N} &= k_{N,\mathrm{syn},0} + k_{N,\mathrm{syn},2}\,u_2 \nonumber\\
          &\quad - k_{\mathrm{PARP}}\max\!\big(0, E - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)N - k_{N,\mathrm{use}}\,N, \label{eq:ode_N} \\
  \dot{C} &= (k_{\mathrm{oct},0} + k_{\mathrm{oct},2}\,u_2)(1.30 - C) \nonumber\\
          &\quad - k_{\mathrm{ox}}\max\!\big(0, E - \theta_{\mathrm{cys}}\big)C, \label{eq:ode_C} \\
  \dot{M}_8 &= k_{\mathrm{drift}}\,E\,(10.0 - M_8) \nonumber\\
            &\quad - k_{\mathrm{TET}}\,u_2\,N\,C\,M_8. \label{eq:ode_M8}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Observation Model and Structural Observability Collinearity.}
Because intracellular masses $(W, D)$ and bottleneck burden $B$ are not directly measurable at real-time sampling rates on an embedded processor, the controller observes a discrete-time four-channel proxy vector $\mathbf{y}_k \in \mathbb{R}^4$ at sampling instants $t_k$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{y}_k = \mathbf{h}_{\mathrm{obs}}\big(\mathbf{x}(t_k)\big) + \mathbf{v}_k =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    E(t_k) \\
    \mathrm{ATP}\big(\mathbf{x}(t_k)\big) \\
    G(t_k) \\
    N(t_k)
  \end{bmatrix}
  + \mathbf{v}_k,
  \label{eq:observation_model}
\end{equation}
where $y_{k,1}$ tracks filtered energetic impedance/stress potential, $y_{k,2}$ is a respirometric or metabolic ATP proxy, $y_{k,3}$ is secreted stress readout, and $y_{k,4}$ is optical $\text{NAD(P)H}$ autofluorescence. Crucially, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:aux_atp}--\eqref{eq:aux_ep} imply $\mathrm{EP}\cdot\mathrm{ATP}/f_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 = 1 + \beta_B B + \beta_h h$: at any single snapshot $t_k$, $B$ and $h$ enter the static observation map only as a linear combination, while $(W, D)$ and $C$ exhibit multiplicative iso-ATP trade-offs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aux_atp} (and $M_8$ is a purely downstream readout with zero feedback into states $x_0\dots x_6$). Reconstructing $(\hat{W}, \hat{D}, \hat{B})$ therefore relies on multi-step dynamic separation via a 7-state Huber-regularized extended Kalman filter (Huber-EKF) \cite{kalman1960new,huber1964robust}, whose local Observability Gramian conditioning and sensitivity to $\pm 20\%$ plant-model mismatch we quantify explicitly in \S5.2.

\subsection{Exact Quotient Flow and Conditional Safety Guarantees}
Applying the quotient rule $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{D}{W+D}\right) = \frac{\dot{D}W - D\dot{W}}{(W+D)^2}$ to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_D} yields the exact heteroplasmy quotient ODE. Specifically, the ROS conversion term $+k_{\mathrm{dmg}}W\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$ in $\dot{D}$ contributes $\frac{W^2}{(W+D)^2}k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}) = (1-h)^2 k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$, while the corresponding loss term $-k_{\mathrm{dmg}}W\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$ in $\dot{W}$ contributes $+\frac{DW}{(W+D)^2}k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}) = h(1-h)k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$. Summing both contributions yields $(1-h)k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP}-\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$, giving:
\begin{align}
  \dot{h} &= -\lambda_h(t)\,h(1-h) \nonumber\\
          &\quad + (1-h)\,k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\,\max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big), \label{eq:h_dot} \\
  \lambda_h(t) &= k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} \nonumber\\
               &\quad - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)\,k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\left(1 - \frac{M(t)}{K}\right). \label{eq:lambda_h}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} reveals why concurrent or premature biogenesis ($u_2 = 1$) can fail when $k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} > 1$ and $B(t)$ is elevated: increasing $k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)$ directly subtracts $(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)k_{\mathrm{bio}}(1 - M/K)$ from the selective contraction rate $\lambda_h(t)$, while elevated $B(t)$ depresses $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B)$ and raises $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})$ above $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$.

We now prove joint forward invariance of the Stage-1 safe box via Nagumo's subtangent theorem, dynamically establishing $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, (1,1,0)) < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ rather than assuming it \textit{a priori}, and resolving the exact boundary vs.\ interior dependence on $B_0$.

\begin{lemma}[Joint Stage-1 Safe-Box Invariance]
\label{lem:invariance}
Under Stage~1 control ($u_0 = 1, u_1 = 1, u_2 = 0$), Eqs.~\eqref{eq:aux_kbio}--\eqref{eq:aux_kmitod} give identically $k_{\mathrm{bio}}(0, N) = k_{\mathrm{bio},0}$ and $k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(1) = k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}$. For $D_0 \in (0, W^*)$ and $B_0 \ge B_{\mathrm{eq}} = b_{\mathrm{basal}}/k_{\mathrm{clr}}$, define the invariant wild-type floor $W_{\min}(D_0) \triangleq K(1 - k_{\mathrm{mito},W}/k_{\mathrm{bio},0}) - D_0 = W^* - D_0$, the candidate compact box $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0) \triangleq \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^8 : W \in [W^* - D_0, K],\, D \in [0, D_0],\, B \in [B_{\mathrm{eq}}, B_0],\, E \in [0, E_{\max}],\, C \in [C_{\min}, 1.30]\}$, and the boundary vs.\ interior critical bottleneck thresholds:
\begin{align}
  B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* &\triangleq K_B\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}}{k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\,(1 - W^*/K)} - 1\right), \label{eq:B_crit} \\
  B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}} &\triangleq K_B\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}}{k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\,\big(1 - \frac{W^* - D_0}{K}\big)} - 1\right). \label{eq:B_crit_mono}
\end{align}
Suppose $\sup_{\Omega_1} \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, (1,1,0)) \le \min(\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}, E_{\max})$ and $k_{\mathrm{oct},0}(1.30 - C_{\min}) \ge k_{\mathrm{ox}}\max(0, E_{\max} - \theta_{\mathrm{cys}})C_{\min}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Joint Forward Invariance ($B_0 \le B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$):} The compact set $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ is positively invariant under Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_C}. In particular, for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}(t), (1,1,0)) \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$, $D(t) \le D_0$, and $W(t)$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    W(t) \ge W_{\min}(D_0) = W^* - D_0.
    \label{eq:W_min}
  \end{equation}
  Moreover, once $B(t) \le B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}}$, $\dot{D}(t) \le 0$ holds pointwise throughout the entire interior of $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$.
  \item \textbf{Super-Critical Bottleneck Dwell Time ($B_0 > B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$):} Since $\dot{B} = b_{\mathrm{basal}} - k_{\mathrm{clr}}B$ under $u_0 = 1$, $B(t)$ reaches $B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$ at the finite dwell time
  \begin{equation}
    t_0 = \frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{clr}}}\ln\!\left(\frac{B_0 - B_{\mathrm{eq}}}{B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* - B_{\mathrm{eq}}}\right),
    \label{eq:t0_dwell}
  \end{equation}
  with $D(t_0) \le \bar{D}(t_0) \triangleq \min\!\big(K, D_0 \exp(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}k_{\mathrm{bio},0} t_0)\big)$, after which Case~1 invariance holds on $\Omega_1(\bar{D}(t_0), B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*)$ whenever $\bar{D}(t_0) < W^*$ and $\mathrm{EP} \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We verify Nagumo's subtangent condition on every boundary face of $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$. First, by assumption $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, (1,1,0)) \le \min(\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}, E_{\max})$ everywhere on $\Omega_1$, so the ROS damage term $\max(0, \mathrm{EP} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}) \equiv 0$ on $\Omega_1$. On the $E$, $C$, and $B$ faces: $\dot{E}|_{E=0} = k_E \mathrm{EP} \ge 0$, $\dot{E}|_{E=E_{\max}} = k_E(\mathrm{EP} - E_{\max}) \le 0$; $\dot{C}|_{C=1.30} \le 0$, $\dot{C}|_{C=C_{\min}} \ge 0$; and under $u_0 = 1$, $\dot{B}|_{B=B_{\mathrm{eq}}} = 0$ and $\dot{B}|_{B=B_0} = k_{\mathrm{clr}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}} - B_0) \le 0$. Crucially, on the upper mutant face $D = D_0$, every point in $\Omega_1$ satisfies $W \ge W^* - D_0$, so total mass on that face satisfies $M = W + D_0 \ge W^*$ automatically (without assuming $M(t) \ge W^*$ in the interior). Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ode_D} at $D = D_0$ therefore gives $\dot{D}|_{D=D_0} \le D_0\big[k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}k_{\mathrm{bio},0}(1 - W^*/K) - k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}\frac{K_B}{K_B + B}\big] \le 0$ for all $B \le B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$, while $\dot{D}|_{D=0} = 0$. On the lower wild-type face $W = W^* - D_0$, every point in $\Omega_1$ satisfies $D \le D_0$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:ode_W} gives
\begin{equation}
  \dot{W}\big|_{W_{\min}} \ge W_{\min}\!\left[k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\!\left(1 - \frac{W^*}{K}\right) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W}\right] = 0
  \label{eq:W_comparison}
\end{equation}
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:kbio0_calibration}, and $\dot{W}|_{W=K} \le -k_{\mathrm{mito},W}K < 0$. Thus $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ is jointly forward invariant. Throughout the interior of $\Omega_1$, $M = W + D \ge W^* - D_0$, so $B(t) \le B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}}$ implies $\dot{D}(t) \le 0$ everywhere on $\Omega_1$. Integrating $\dot{B} = b_{\mathrm{basal}} - k_{\mathrm{clr}}B$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:t0_dwell}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact Verification on the Canonical Neuronal Box.}
Set $\Omega_1$ with $D_0 = 0.400$, $W \in [0.600, 1.250]$, $B \in [0.04001, 0.500]$, $E_{\max} = 1.350$, and $C_{\min} = 0.650$. Under $(u_0, u_1, u_2) = (1, 1, 0)$, $f_{\mathrm{eff}}(1,0)^2 = (1 - 0.220)^2 = 0.6084$. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:aux_er}, $\mathrm{EP}$ decreases strictly with $W$ and $C$ and increases with $B$; fixing $(W, B, C) = (0.600, 0.500, 0.650)$, $\phi(D) = \frac{1.125 + 0.65 D/(0.60+D)}{0.60 + 0.15 D} = \frac{0.675 + 1.775 D}{0.36 + 0.69 D + 0.15 D^2}$ has numerator derivative $0.17325 - 0.2025 D - 0.26625 D^2 > 0$ for all $D \in [0, 0.400]$. Hence $\mathrm{EP}$ attains its exact global maximum on $\Omega_1$ at $(0.600, 0.400, 0.500, 0.650)$:
\begin{align*}
  \max_{\Omega_1} \mathrm{EP}\big(\mathbf{x}, (1,1,0)\big) &= \frac{1.385}{0.660 \times 1.221}\times 0.6084 \\
  &= 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150,
\end{align*}
while $\dot{C}|_{C=0.650} \ge 0.040(0.650) - 0.050(0.500)(0.650) = +0.00975 > 0$. Equations~\eqref{eq:B_crit}--\eqref{eq:B_crit_mono} give $B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* = 0.250(5.8081 - 1) = 1.2020 > B_0 = 0.500$ and $B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}} = 0.250(2.2340 - 1) = 0.3085$ (crossed by $B(t)$ at $t = 3.16\text{ d}$), confirming $W(t) \ge W_{\min}(0.40) = 0.600 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$ for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$. For super-critical $B_0 = 2.00 > 1.2020$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:t0_dwell} gives $t_0 = 2.91\text{ d}$.

\begin{proposition}[Analytical Regional Contraction]
\label{prop:stability}
Let $\mathbf{x}(0) \in \Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} Case~1 ($B_0 \le B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Closed-Form Uniform Contraction Floor $\lambda_{\min}$:} On the invariant set $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$, $B(t) \le B_0$ and $M(t) = W(t) + D(t) \ge W^* - D_0$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} under Stage~1 ($u_1 = 1, u_2 = 0$) is bounded below analytically for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$ by
  \begin{align}
    \lambda_{\min} &\triangleq \frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}K_B}{K_B + B_0} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} \nonumber\\
                   &\quad - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\!\left(1 - \frac{W^* - D_0}{K}\right). \label{eq:lambda_min_analytic}
  \end{align}
  Whenever $\lambda_{\min} > 0$ (for the canonical neuronal box, $\lambda_{\min} = \frac{0.115}{3} - 0.015 - 0.32(0.075)(0.520) = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1} > 0$), $V(h) = \frac{1}{2}h^2$ satisfies $\dot{V} = -\lambda_h(t)h^2(1-h) \le -\lambda_{\min}(1 - h_0)h^2$, yielding both the logistic and regional exponential bounds:
  \begin{align}
    h(t) &\le \frac{h_0\,e^{-\lambda_{\min} t}}{1 - h_0 + h_0\,e^{-\lambda_{\min} t}} \nonumber\\
         &\le h_0\exp\!\big(-\lambda_{\min}(1 - h_0)t\big). \label{eq:prop1_bounds}
  \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Sub-Damage Transcritical Invasion Exchange:} At the mutant-free manifold $(W^*, 0, B_{\mathrm{eq}})$ where $1 - W^*/K = k_{\mathrm{mito},W}/k_{\mathrm{bio}}$, the linearized mutant invasion eigenvalue is $\mu_D = -\lambda_h^* = k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}})$. Whenever $k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}}) < k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{mito},W}$, $h^* = 0$ is unstable ($\mu_D > 0$); Stage~1 mitophagy satisfying $k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}}) > k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{mito},W}$ renders $\mu_D < 0$ and $h^* = 0$ locally exponentially stable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ and $\mathrm{EP}(t) \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:h_dot} reduces to $\dot{h} = -\lambda_h(t)h(1-h)$, and substituting $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(t)) \ge \frac{K_B}{K_B + B_0}$ and $1 - M(t)/K \le 1 - (W^* - D_0)/K$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_min_analytic}. Separating variables gives $\frac{d}{dt}\ln\left(\frac{h}{1-h}\right) = -\lambda_h(t) \le -\lambda_{\min}$, which integrates to the first inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prop1_bounds}; since $h(t) \le h_0$ on $[0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, $1 - h(t) \ge 1 - h_0$, giving $\dot{h} \le -\lambda_{\min}(1 - h_0)h$ and the second inequality. Linearizing $\dot{D}$ around $(W^*, 0, B_{\mathrm{eq}})$ gives $\dot{D} \approx \mu_D D$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unconstrained Discrete-Adjoint Optimal Control vs.\ Threshold Feedback}
To evaluate control policies over $t \in [0, T_{\max}]$ identically to \texttt{compute\_bolza\_cost} in \texttt{simulator/\allowbreak generate\_\allowbreak paper1\_\allowbreak verified\_\allowbreak benchmarks.py}, we define the finite-horizon Bolza objective functional:
\begin{align}
  J(\mathbf{u}) &= w_h\,h(T_{\max})^2 + w_W\big(W_{\mathrm{ref}} - W(T_{\max})\big)^2 \nonumber\\
                &\quad + \int_0^{T_{\max}} \Big[ w_{\mathrm{EP}}\,\max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP}(t) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)^2 \nonumber\\
                &\qquad + w_{\mathrm{surv}}\,\max\!\big(0, \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} - \mathrm{ATP}(t)\big)^2 \nonumber\\
                &\qquad + \frac{w_{\mathrm{def}}}{T_{\max}}\,\max\!\big(0, 0.85 - W(t)\big)^2 \nonumber\\
                &\qquad + r_1 u_1(t) + r_2 u_2(t) \Big] dt,
  \label{eq:bolza_cost}
\end{align}
with terminal weights $w_h = 80, w_W = 30$ ($W_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1.00$), safety penalty weights $w_{\mathrm{EP}} = 180\text{ d}^{-1}, w_{\mathrm{surv}} = 250\text{ d}^{-1}, w_{\mathrm{def}} = 0.85$, and horizon-normalized actuation weights $r_1 = 1.15/T_{\max}, r_2 = 0.12/T_{\max}$ (with $r_0 = 0$, representing non-penalized housekeeping bottleneck clearance so $\partial\mathcal{H}/\partial u_0 \le 0 \implies u_0^*(t) \equiv 1$).

\paragraph{Discrete-Adjoint Pontryagin Solution and Interior Constraint Arc.}
In Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_M8}, $(u_1, u_2)$ enter linearly in the actuation penalty $r_1 u_1 + r_2 u_2$ and in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:aux_kbio}--\eqref{eq:aux_kmitod}, but enter \textit{quadratically} in $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) = \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x})(1 - c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2)^2$ whenever $\mathrm{EP}$ approaches $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$, while $u_2$ also couples dynamically through cofactor $N(t)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ode_N}. Monotonicity of $h(t)$ therefore neither establishes bang-bang optimality nor excludes interior state-constraint arcs.

To resolve the true optimal trajectory without structural assumptions, we discretize $\mathbf{u}(t) \in [0, 1]^3$ into $N_{\mathrm{ctrl}} = 100$ free piecewise-constant control intervals ($300$ decision variables over $1{,}000$ exact \texttt{RK4} intervals on $[0, 90\text{ d}]$), propagate the exact backward-in-time discrete \texttt{RK4} adjoint equations to obtain $\nabla_{u} J$ (verified against central finite differences to directional relative error $1.95 \times 10^{-9}$), and solve $\min_{u \in [0,1]^{300}} J(\mathbf{u})$ via \texttt{L-BFGS-B}. Across five diverse initializations (staged relay, all-zero, uniform midpoint $0.5$, build-first, and uniform random), \texttt{L-BFGS-B} converges to the same optimum within a $0.12\%$ cost spread ($J \in [0.72620, 0.72709]$, interior-arc stationarity residual $\max |\partial J/\partial u_{1,m}| = 1.47 \times 10^{-3}$). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}A, the unconstrained optimum achieves $J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$ ($h(T_{\max}) = 0.0300, W(T_{\max}) = 0.9938, \mathrm{EP}_{\max} = 1.150$) via two non-relay features:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Early Interior Redox-Boundary Modulation ($t \in [0, 17\text{ d}]$):} Rather than saturating $u_1 = 1$, $u_1^*(t)$ modulates smoothly across $[0.44, 0.83]$ so that $\mathrm{EP}(t)$ rides just along $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$ without incurring unnecessary $r_1 u_1$ effort, ramping to $u_1^* \approx 1.0$ during peak selection ($t \in [20, 33\text{ d}]$) before tapering to zero by $t \approx 75\text{ d}$.
  \item \textbf{Late Terminal Biogenesis Sprint ($t \in [78, 90\text{ d}]$):} $u_2^*(t)$ remains identically zero until $h^*(t) < 0.030$, then executes a concentrated terminal burst to reach $W(T_{\max}) = 0.994$ while minimizing replicative expansion $(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)k_{\mathrm{bio}}D$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Embedded State-Feedback Threshold Law and Optimality Decomposition.}
While $u^*(t)$ minimizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:bolza_cost} in open loop, riding $\mathrm{EP}^*(t) \equiv \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$ leaves zero robustness margin against kinetic mismatch or mid-horizon insults. For embedded closed-loop control, we trigger the Stage~1 $\to$ Stage~2 relay transition ($(u_0, u_1, u_2) : (1, 1, 0) \to (1, u_{1,\mathrm{S2}}, 1)$ with $u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} = 0.35$) when $B(t) \le B_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.120$ and $h(t) \le h_{\mathrm{gate}}$ (\texttt{coralnpu\_core8\_stage\_gate\_q16}). By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:aux_er}--\eqref{eq:aux_ep}, switching raises $f_{\mathrm{eff}}^2$ from $(1 - c_1)^2 = 0.6084$ to $(1 - c_1 u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} + c_2)^2 = 0.9274$, requiring:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{EP}(t_{\mathrm{gate}}^+) &= \frac{1 + \beta_B B(t_{\mathrm{gate}}) + \beta_h h_{\mathrm{gate}}}{W(t_{\mathrm{gate}})\big(1 + \eta_D\frac{h_{\mathrm{gate}}}{1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}}}\big)\big(1 + \gamma_C C(t_{\mathrm{gate}})\big)} \nonumber\\
  &\quad \times (1 - c_1 u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} + c_2)^2 \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}.
  \label{eq:hgate_derivation}
\end{align}
At $h_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$ ($t_{\mathrm{gate}} = 33.30\text{ d}$, $W = 0.772, B = 0.041, C = 1.091$), $\mathrm{EP}(t_{\mathrm{gate}}^+)$ jumps from $0.603$ to $0.919 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$, preserving a $20.1\%$ redox safety margin (and remaining $\le 1.031 < 1.150$ even at unrecovered $C_0 = 0.65$). Conversely, for $h_{\mathrm{gate}} \ge 0.195$, $\mathrm{EP}(t_{\mathrm{gate}}^+)$ breaches $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$ and causes $J(h_{\mathrm{gate}})$ to spike sharply ($0.887 \to 3.120$, Figure~\ref{fig:ode_control}B). Comparing $J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$, the optimal two-stage constant relay $J_{\mathrm{2stage}}^* = 0.850$ ($t_{\mathrm{sw}}^* = 48.18\text{ d}, u_{1,\mathrm{S2}}^* = 0$), and $J_{\mathrm{thresh}}(0.08) = 0.8875$ decomposes the suboptimality into a $+17.05\%$ structural relay-restriction cost and a $+4.38\%$ constant-threshold penalty ($+22.21\%$ total vs.\ $J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^*$). Scaling the mitophagy weight $r_1 \leftarrow \alpha_{r_1} r_1$ across $\alpha_{r_1} \in \{0.25, 1.00, 4.00\}$ yields $(J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^*, J_{\mathrm{thresh}}) = (0.2231, 0.3782)$, $(0.7262, 0.8875)$, and $(2.4121, 2.9247)$ ($+69.46\%, +22.21\%, +21.25\%$ relative gaps), showing that the relative gap widens at very small $r_1$ due to fixed Stage-2 maintenance $u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} = 0.35$.

Evaluating this depleted point ($W_0 = 0.17, D_0 = 0.38$, initial $\mathrm{ATP}(0) = 0.474 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$), immediate Purge-First removes ATP-producing $D$ faster than basal biogenesis replaces $W$, dipping transiently to $\mathrm{ATP}_{\min} = 0.440 < 0.450$, whereas an 18-day Build-First pulse preserves $\mathrm{ATP}_{\min} = 0.474 \ge 0.450$.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Zone~0 --- Irreversible Bioenergetic Collapse ($182/961 = 18.9\%$):} At severely depleted $W_0$, initial energetic deficit violates $\mathrm{ATP}(t) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$ or prevents recovery ($W(T_{\max}) < 0.85$) under \textit{both} Build-First and Purge-Then-Build, defining the fundamental controllability limit.

  \item \textbf{Zone~2 --- Purge-Then-Build Exclusive Rescue ($59/961 = 6.1\%$):} At stronger ROS coupling ($k_{\mathrm{dmg}} \ge 0.023, W_0 \in [0.18, 0.28]$), Build-First drives $\mathrm{EP}(t) > \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ and triggers runaway wild-type loss, whereas Purge-Then-Build rescues the cell.
  \item \textbf{Zone~3 --- Both Safe, Purge-Then-Build Cost-Optimal ($692/961 = 72.0\%$):} Both policies satisfy $\mathrm{ATP}(t) \ge 0.450$, with Purge-Then-Build achieving strictly lower Bolza cost.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Accumulated-Selection Reduction and Multi-Term Perturbation Bound}

A natural question is whether Stage-1 heteroplasmy clearance across biological systems with vastly different turnover rates (hours in activated T~cells vs.\ weeks in flight muscle vs.\ months in post-mitotic neurons) can be analyzed in a common dimensionless coordinate. As noted in control-theoretic critiques of naive Buckingham $\Pi$ reductions \cite{buckingham1914physically}, dimensional analysis alone cannot reduce an 8-state ODE to a 1D curve unless the time-variation of the contraction rate $\lambda_h(t)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} is explicitly accounted for. We therefore establish both (i) the exact accumulated-selection identity and (ii) a complete multi-term perturbation bound for constant-rate rescaling.

\begin{proposition}[Accumulated-Selection Reduction]
\label{prop:reduction}
Suppose $\mathrm{EP}(t) \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ on $[0, t]$ in Stage~1 ($u_0=1$, $u_1=1$, $u_2=0$) with $h_0 \in (0, 1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact Accumulated-Selection Coordinate $s(t)$:} Define the dimensionless accumulated selection integral
  \begin{equation}
    s(t) \triangleq \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq.
    \label{eq:s_accum}
  \end{equation}
  Then the 8-state ODE heteroplasmy trajectory $h(t)$ satisfies the exact 1D logistic identity
  \begin{equation}
    h(t) = h_{\mathrm{logistic}}\big(s(t); h_0\big) \triangleq \frac{h_0\,e^{-s(t)}}{1 - h_0 + h_0\,e^{-s(t)}},
    \label{eq:h_logistic_exact}
  \end{equation}
  and reaches any target threshold $h_{\mathrm{gate}} \in (0, h_0)$ at the exact accumulated-selection value
  \begin{equation}
    s^*(h_0, h_{\mathrm{gate}}) = \ln\!\left(\frac{h_0(1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}})}{h_{\mathrm{gate}}(1 - h_0)}\right),
    \label{eq:s_star}
  \end{equation}
  which depends explicitly on $(h_0, h_{\mathrm{gate}})$ (giving $s^*(0.40, 0.08) = \ln(4.6/0.6) = 2.03688$, $s^*(0.25, 0.08) = 1.34373$, and $s^*(0.60, 0.08) = 2.84781$).
  \item \textbf{Zero-Tuning Closed-Form Perturbation Bound for Constant-Rate Rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$:} Because $u_2 \equiv 0$ during Stage~1, Eq.~\eqref{eq:aux_kbio} gives $k_{\mathrm{bio}}(0, N) \equiv k_{\mathrm{bio},0}$ identically (zero cofactor rate error), while $\dot{B} = b_{\mathrm{basal}} - k_{\mathrm{clr}}B$ integrates in closed form to $B(q) = B_{\mathrm{eq}} + (B_0 - B_{\mathrm{eq}})e^{-k_{\mathrm{clr}}q}$. Let $T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}} \triangleq s^*(h_0, h_{\mathrm{gate}})\big/\big[k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}}) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}(1 - M_0/K)\big]$ be the cleared-bottleneck horizon estimate, and define the \textit{a priori} Stage-1 mean lysosomal competence $\bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}} = V_{\mathrm{lys}}(\bar{B})$ with zero empirical tuning constants:
  \begin{align}
    \bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}} &\triangleq \frac{1}{T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}}\int_0^{T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}} \frac{K_B\,dq}{K_B + B(q)} \nonumber\\
    &= \frac{K_B}{K_B + B_{\mathrm{eq}}}\!\left[1 - \frac{\ln\!\left(\frac{K_B + B_0}{K_B + B(T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}})}\right)}{k_{\mathrm{clr}}\,T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}}\right], \label{eq:vlys_bar_closed} \\
    \hat{\lambda}_0 &\triangleq k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}\,\bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} \nonumber\\
    &\quad - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\left(1 - \frac{M_0}{K}\right). \label{eq:lambda_hat0}
  \end{align}
  Let $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$, $\ell(t) = \ln\frac{h(t)}{1-h(t)}$, and $\hat{\ell}(\hat{\tau}) = \ln\frac{h_0}{1-h_0} - \hat{\tau}$. Then for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, $|\ell(t) - \hat{\ell}(\hat{\lambda}_0 t)| = |s(t) - \hat{\lambda}_0 t|$ satisfies the two-term perturbation inequality:
  \begin{align}
    \big|s(t) - \hat{\lambda}_0 t\big| &\le \underbrace{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}\!\int_0^t \!\big|V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(q)) - \bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}}\big|\,dq}_{\mathcal{E}_B(t)\;\mathrm{(bottleneck)}} \nonumber\\
    &\quad + \underbrace{(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)\frac{k_{\mathrm{bio},0}}{K}\!\int_0^t \!\big|M(q) - M_0\big|\,dq}_{\mathcal{E}_M(t)\;\mathrm{(mass,\;>0\;at\;}B_0=0\mathrm{)}},
    \label{eq:multi_term_bound}
  \end{align}
  and since $\left|\frac{d}{d\ell}\frac{e^\ell}{1+e^\ell}\right| = h(1-h) \le \frac{1}{4}$, the heteroplasmy supremum error satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \big|h(t) - h_{\mathrm{logistic}}(\hat{\lambda}_0 t; h_0)\big| \le \frac{1}{4}\Big(\mathcal{E}_B(t) + \mathcal{E}_M(t)\Big).
    \label{eq:sup_err_bound}
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~1 follows by integrating $\frac{d}{dt}\ln\left(\frac{h}{1-h}\right) = -\lambda_h(t)$ over $[0, t]$ and inverting the logit map. For Part~2, substituting $u_1 = 1, u_2 = 0$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} eliminates $N(q)$ identically; integrating $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(q))$ via $w = e^{k_{\mathrm{clr}}q}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:vlys_bar_closed}. Subtracting Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_hat0} from Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h}, applying the triangle inequality (noting also $|V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) - V_{\mathrm{lys}}(\bar{B})| \le |B - \bar{B}|/K_B$), and bounding the logistic derivative by $\max_{h \in [0,1]} h(1-h) = 1/4$ proves Eqs.~\eqref{eq:multi_term_bound}--\eqref{eq:sup_err_bound}. Notably, $\mathcal{E}_M(t)$ remains strictly positive even when $B_0 = B_{\mathrm{eq}} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Target Architecture and Single-Bank L1 DTCM Memory Budget}
We target the open-source Google Coral NPU architecture (\texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x}) \cite{coralnpu2025datasheet,waterman2014riscv}, which combines a 32-bit RISC-V scalar core with a 128-bit single-instruction multiple-data (SIMD) vector execution unit (\texttt{Zve32x} extension supporting four 32-bit lanes or eight 16-bit lanes per register), $8\text{ KB}$ of instruction tightly coupled memory (ITCM), and $32\text{ KB}$ of data tightly coupled memory (DTCM) organized as four $8{,}192\text{-byte}$ SRAM banks.

To minimize static leakage power during always-on chronic bioelectronic monitoring, our embedded design constrains the entire runtime data footprint (INT8 projection LUTs, mixed \texttt{Q16.16}/\texttt{Q8.24} 7-state Huber-EKF covariance matrices, \texttt{RK4} slope registers, 64-frame sensor ring buffers, and scalar/ISR stack) to a static C99 struct \texttt{coralnpu\_dtcm\_bank0\_workspace\_t} verified at compile and run time to occupy \textbf{$3{,}696\text{ B}$ ($45.12\%$) of a single $8{,}192\text{-byte}$ L1 DTCM bank (\texttt{Bank~0}, leaving $4{,}496\text{ B}$ free headroom)}, allowing the remaining three $8\text{ KB}$ DTCM banks ($24\text{ KB}$) to remain power-gated in deep retention mode (Figure~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}D and Table~\ref{tab:memory_power}).

\subsection{Scalar \texttt{FP32} vs.\ \texttt{Zve32x} Integer SIMD, Fixed-Point Huber-EKF, and Deadband-Free \texttt{RK4}}
An explicit architectural nuance of \texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x} merits clarification: the scalar RISC-V core includes a single-lane IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point unit (\texttt{F} extension, \texttt{FP32}), whereas its 128-bit vector unit implements \textbf{integer-only} \texttt{Zve32x} (supporting four parallel 32-bit integer lanes or eight 16-bit lanes, without vector floating-point \texttt{Zve32f}). Consequently, scalar single-compartment integration can execute directly in \texttt{FP32}, whereas vectorizing multi-lane state updates across the 128-bit SIMD datapath requires signed 32-bit \texttt{Q16.16} fixed-point arithmetic ($1.0 = 65{,}536$, resolution $2^{-16} \approx 1.526 \times 10^{-5}$).

In \texttt{build/coralnpu/\allowbreak mitorestorer\_coralnpu\_kernel.h}, exact firmware parity across multi-week biological timescales combines three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Bit-Exact 64-Bit Widening Vector Stage Updates:} Multiplying \texttt{Q16.16} operands or scaling \texttt{Q8.24} slopes by $\Delta t_{\mathrm{Q16}}$ produces 64-bit intermediates (\texttt{Q32.32} or \texttt{Q24.40}). Both \texttt{coralnpu\_q24\_axpy8\_q16} (inside our 4-stage \texttt{RK4} stepper) and \texttt{coralnpu\_rvv\_rk4\_bioenergetic\_step} execute 32-to-64-bit widening vector multiplication (\texttt{\_\_riscv\_vwmul\_vx\_i64m4}) followed by narrowing arithmetic right shift (\texttt{\_\_riscv\_vnsra\_wx\_i32m2(..., 24)} or \texttt{16}). Across $256$ randomized 8-lane trials ($2{,}048$ lane operations), widening vector execution achieves \textbf{0-ULP discrepancy} against scalar 64-bit arithmetic.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Q8.24} Slope Scaling and 8-Lane $\Sigma\Delta$ Sub-LSB Remainder Diffusion:} Because slow biological rates ($k_{\mathrm{mito},W} = 0.015\text{ d}^{-1}$) multiplied by refined step sizes $\Delta t \le 0.045\text{ d}$ produce per-step increments smaller than $0.5\text{ LSB}_{16}$ ($7.63 \times 10^{-6}$), naive \texttt{Q16.16} Runge-Kutta integration suffers complete quantization deadband stalling as $\Delta t \to 0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}D). Even evaluating stage derivatives $\mathbf{k}_1\dots\mathbf{k}_4$ in left-shifted \texttt{Q8.24} format ($2^{-24} \approx 5.96 \times 10^{-8}$) still hits the $\pm 0.5\text{ LSB}_{16}$ state-update rounding deadband when $\Delta t$ is small. To break this barrier, \texttt{coralnpu\_rk4\_core8\_full\_step\_q16} accumulates the exact 64-bit weighted numerator $\Delta t \cdot (\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) + \mathbf{r}_{k-1}$ against divisor $6 \times 2^{24} = 100{,}663{,}296$ and propagates the signed sub-LSB remainder vector $\mathbf{r}_k \in (-100{,}663{,}296,\, 100{,}663{,}296)^8$ across steps as a first-order $\Sigma\Delta$ error-diffusion accumulator.
  \item \textbf{Bare-Metal Mixed \texttt{Q16.16}/\texttt{Q8.24} 7-State Sequential Scalar Huber-EKF:} To execute closed-loop estimation without dynamic memory (\texttt{malloc}) or \texttt{<math.h>} floating-point library calls, \texttt{coralnpu\_huber\_ekf7\_meas\_update\_q16} stores state estimates $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0:6}$ in \texttt{Q16.16} and covariance $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{Z}_{32}^{7\times 7}$ in \texttt{Q8.24} (preserving variance resolution down to $2^{-24} \approx 6 \times 10^{-8}$), processes the four channels of $\mathbf{y}_k$ sequentially via scalar innovation variances $S_i = \mathbf{h}_i \mathbf{P} \mathbf{h}_i^\top + R_i$ (avoiding $4 \times 4$ matrix inversion), extracts innovation standard deviations via exact 64-bit integer Newton square root (\texttt{coralnpu\_isqrt64}), and applies symmetric Huber weight scaling $w_{\mathrm{Hub}} = \min(1, \gamma_{\mathrm{Huber}}\sqrt{S_i}/|\nu_i|)$ directly to both state and covariance updates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{64-Frame Dual-Modality Persistence Filter Under Markov Correlation}
To prevent transient sensor artifacts from prematurely triggering Stage-2 biogenesis or emergency rollback, the controller evaluates a 64-frame sliding bitmask register $\mathcal{B}_k \in \{0,1\}^{64}$ on primary stress channel $y_{k,1}$ with quorum threshold $M_{\mathrm{req}} = 60$, gated by a concurrent secondary modality check $z_k = y_{k,2} \ge \gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}$ (\texttt{coralnpu\_rolling\_fuzzy\_sensor\_gate}).

\begin{proposition}[Markov Persistence Bound]
\label{prop:persistence}
Suppose stationary sensor noise evolves via the reversible Gaussian $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ Markov chain $e_k = \rho\,e_{k-1} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\,\epsilon_k$ on $\mathbb{R}$ ($\epsilon_k \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $\rho \in [0, 1)$, stationary measure $\pi = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$), whose Markov operator possesses $L^2(\pi)$ absolute spectral gap $\gamma_{\mathrm{spec}} = 1 - \rho > 0$. Let $I_k = f(e_k) \triangleq \mathbb{I}(y_{k,1}(e_k) \ge \gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}) \in \{0, 1\}$ be the bounded measurable primary exceedance indicator with marginal expectation $p_y = \mathbb{E}_\pi[I_k] < \mu_{\mathrm{req}} \triangleq M_{\mathrm{req}}/N_{\mathrm{win}} = 60/64 = 0.9375$, let $p_{z|Q} \triangleq \mathbb{P}\big(z_k \ge \gamma_{\mathrm{sec}} \;\big|\; \sum_{j=k-63}^k I_j \ge 60\big)$ denote the quorum-conditioned secondary exceedance probability, and let $c_0 \in \{0, \dots, 64\}$ denote the exceedance count in the 64-frame ring buffer immediately prior to a contiguous deterministic primary disturbance of length $L_{\mathrm{dist}}$ frames. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic Sub-Quorum Rejection Condition:} Reaching primary quorum $\sum_{j=k-63}^k I_j \ge 60$ during the disturbance is deterministically impossible whenever
  \begin{equation}
    L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59 - c_0.
    \label{eq:deterministic_cutoff}
  \end{equation}
  Under a clean prior buffer ($c_0 = 0$), zero false triggers occur for $L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59$ frames; with $c_0 > 0$ prior noise exceedances, quorum requires $L_{\mathrm{dist}} \ge 60 - c_0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}B).
  \item \textbf{Spectral-Gap Concentration and Horizon Union Bound:} Unlike classical Hoeffding bounds requiring independent samples \cite{hoeffding1963probability}, applying Paulin's concentration inequality for bounded functions $f : \mathbb{R} \to [0, 1]$ of reversible Markov chains \cite{paulin2015concentration} to $e_k$ bounds the single-step and $K_{\mathrm{frames}}$-horizon false-trigger probabilities by:
  \begin{align}
    P_{\mathrm{FP,step}} &\le p_{z|Q}\,\exp\!\left(-2\,N_{\mathrm{win}}\,\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\,(\mu_{\mathrm{req}} - p_y)^2\right), \label{eq:paulin_step} \\
    P_{\mathrm{FP,horizon}} &\le \min\!\big(1,\, K_{\mathrm{frames}}\,P_{\mathrm{FP,step}}\big). \label{eq:paulin_horizon}
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Any 64-frame window overlapping a contiguous $L_{\mathrm{dist}}$-frame burst contains at most $c_0 + L_{\mathrm{dist}}$ ones, which is $< 60$ whenever $L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59 - c_0$, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:deterministic_cutoff}. Note that binary thresholded indicators $I_k$ of an $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ process are not themselves a 2-state Markov chain, whereas the latent Gaussian process $e_k$ \textit{is} a stationary reversible Markov chain on $(\mathbb{R}, \pi)$ with Mehler--Hermite spectrum $\{\rho^m\}_{m \ge 1}$ and $L^2(\pi)$ spectral gap $\gamma_{\mathrm{spec}} = 1 - \rho$. Applying Theorem~3.3 of Paulin~\cite{paulin2015concentration} directly to the bounded measurable function $f(e_k) \in [0, 1]$ yields $\mathbb{P}_\pi(\sum_{j=1}^{64} f(e_j) \ge 60) \le \exp(-2 N_{\mathrm{win}}\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}(\mu_{\mathrm{req}} - p_y)^2)$. Multiplying by the exact conditioning factor $p_{z|Q}$ and applying Boole's union bound over $K_{\mathrm{frames}}$ steps yields Eqs.~\eqref{eq:paulin_step}--\eqref{eq:paulin_horizon}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage-1 Observability Gramian and Closed-Loop Huber-EKF Under $\pm 20\%$ Mismatch}
Because state $M_8$ is a purely downstream epigenetic readout ($\partial\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{0:6}/\partial M_8 = \mathbf{0}$, $\partial\mathbf{h}_{\mathrm{obs}}/\partial M_8 = \mathbf{0}$, giving exact singular value $\sigma_8 = 0$), output-feedback estimation operates on the 7-state core $\mathbf{x}_{0:6} = [W, D, B, E, G, N, C]^\top$ from the 4-channel proxy $\mathbf{y}_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:observation_model}. Because the switching decision must be made \textit{prior} to Stage-2 excitation, we evaluate the empirical local Observability Gramian $\mathcal{O}_7$ strictly over the pre-switch Stage-1 window $t \in [0, 33.30\text{ d}]$ ($370$ steps at the exact controller sampling step $\Delta t = 0.09\text{ d}$), yielding singular values $\sigma(\mathcal{O}_7) = [963.44,\, 253.33,\, 205.73,\, 171.19,\, 137.38,\, 15.61,\, 1.29]$ and condition number $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = \sigma_1/\sigma_7 = 745.6$. Consistent with \S2.1, the weakest observable mode ($\sigma_7 = 1.29$, eigenvector $0.980 C - 0.162 W - 0.104 D$) aligns with multiplicative iso-ATP compensation in $(W + \eta_D D)(1 + \gamma_C C)$, while the second-weakest mode ($\sigma_6 = 15.61$, eigenvector $0.978 B - 0.193 D$) reflects $(B, h)$ collinearity in $1 + \beta_B B + \beta_h h$.

We execute a $1{,}000$-step ($\Delta t = 0.09\text{ d}$), 120-trial Monte Carlo benchmark in which every true plant independently draws uniform $\pm 20\%$ perturbations across all 7 core kinetic parameters $(k_{\mathrm{mito},W}, k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}, k_{\mathrm{bio},0}, k_{\mathrm{bio},\max}, k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}, k_{\mathrm{clr}}, K_B)$ plus $\pm 8\%$ initial-state uncertainty unknown to the EKF internal model, corrupted simultaneously by correlated $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ sensor noise ($\rho = 0.60$) and $5\%$ heavy-tailed $6.5\sigma$ measurement spikes (Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}C and Table~\ref{tab:ekf_ablation}A). Using consistent single-weight Huber M-estimation ($w_{\mathrm{Hub}} = \min(1, \gamma_{\mathrm{Huber}}\sqrt{S_i}/|\nu_i|)$ at $\gamma_{\mathrm{Huber}} = 1.50$) and defining a premature switch as latching Stage~2 while $h_{\mathrm{true}} > 0.095$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unfiltered Standard EKF ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$):} Outlier Kalman innovations and mismatch bias yield $2.18\%$ heteroplasmy RMSE and trigger premature Stage-2 transitions in $29/120$ trials ($24.17\%$).
  \item \textbf{Huber-EKF + Persistence ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$):} Single-weight Huber innovation clipping ($\gamma_{\mathrm{Huber}} = 1.50$) with 8-step persistence gating lowers RMSE to $1.95\%$ and reduces premature transitions to $17/120$ ($14.17\%$), leaving only the steady $\sim 1.9\%$ observability-conditioned mismatch bias.
  \item \textbf{Margin-Tightened Huber-EKF ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.065$):} Tightening the observer switching threshold by $0.015$ reduces premature switches by $4.8\times$ relative to Standard EKF to $6/120$ ($5.00\%$, $\hat{h}$ RMSE $1.93\%$), achieving mean terminal $W(T_{\max}) = 1.0479$ and $h(T_{\max}) = 0.0218$ (median $J = 0.933$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Equal-Footing Plant-Preserving Actuator Reversal vs.\ One-Way Latch}
A critical distinction in biological control is that \textbf{a digital microcontroller can restore its internal reference registers $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ckpt}}$, but it cannot reset the physical biological plant state $\mathbf{x}(t)$}. To evaluate actuator rollback on a strictly equal footing ($u_{0,\mathrm{target}} \equiv 1.0$ identical for both policies), we apply a controller-independent exogenous proteostatic/uncoupling insult during Days~$36\text{--}48$ ($\Delta b_{\mathrm{basal}} = +0.52\text{ d}^{-1}$ and acute uncoupling load $c_2 = 0.32$):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Detection Latency ($\delta t_{\mathrm{detect}} = 0.54\text{ d}$):} Once $\mathrm{EP}(t) > \theta_{\mathrm{abort}} = 1.18$ persists across $6$ consecutive controller steps ($0.54\text{ d}$), the controller loads $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ckpt}}$ into its supervisory gating register and commands Stage-2 $\to$ Stage-1 actuator reversal ($u_{2,\mathrm{target}} \leftarrow 0, u_{1,\mathrm{target}} \leftarrow 1$, keeping $u_{0,\mathrm{target}} = 1$).
  \item \textbf{Pharmacokinetic Washout ($\tau_{\mathrm{wash}} = 0.75\text{ d}$):} Physical actuation evolves via $\tau_{\mathrm{wash}}\dot{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{target}} - \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, while the biological plant $\mathbf{x}(t)$ evolves continuously without any discontinuous state reset.
  \item \textbf{Post-Disturbance Excursion Bound ($\Delta h_{\max}$):} Because ROS conversion is active only while $\mathrm{EP}(t) > \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ during the detection plus washout window $\Delta t_{\mathrm{active}} \approx \delta t_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau_{\mathrm{wash}}\ln\!\big(\frac{\mathrm{EP}_{\mathrm{peak}} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}}{\theta_{\mathrm{abort}} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}}\big)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:h_dot} bounds the net heteroplasmy excursion by
  \begin{align}
    \Delta h_{\max} &\le (1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}})\,k_{\mathrm{dmg}} \nonumber\\
                    &\quad \times \int_{t_{\mathrm{insult}}}^{t_{\mathrm{insult}} + \Delta t_{\mathrm{active}}} \max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP}(q) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)\,dq.
    \label{eq:rollback_excursion_bound}
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}C, even with full housekeeping clearance $u_0 \equiv 1$, keeping Stage-2 biogenesis latched on ($u_2 = 1$) during the insult drives $\mathrm{EP}_{\mathrm{peak}} = 11.128 > \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ and causes irreversible ROS-driven collapse ($\Delta h_{\max} = +0.9202$, $\mathrm{ATP}_{\min} = 0.138 < \theta_{\mathrm{surv}}$, terminal $W(T_{\max}) = 0.0001$), whereas plant-preserving actuator reversal caps $\mathrm{EP}_{\mathrm{peak}} = 1.521$, bounds $\Delta h_{\max} = +0.0098$ (well below Eq.~\ref{eq:rollback_excursion_bound}'s analytical bound $0.033$), maintains $\mathrm{ATP}_{\min} = 0.806 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}}$, re-enters Stage~2 once $h(t) \le 0.080$, $B(t) \le 0.120$, and $\mathrm{EP}(t) \le 0.950$, and restores $W(T_{\max}) = 1.0128$ and $h(T_{\max}) = 0.0392$.

\subsection{Host-Emulated Fixed-Point Parity, C99 Huber-EKF, Deadband Ablation, and Power}
Tables~\ref{tab:ekf_ablation}B and~\ref{tab:rk4_parity} and Figures~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}A and~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}D report the live execution of \texttt{build/coralnpu/coralnpu\_hil\_testbench.c} compiled under strict C99 (\texttt{cc -std=c99 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror}). Across $1{,}000$ full-horizon steps ($T_{\max} = 90\text{ d}$), our \texttt{Q16.16} kernel with \texttt{Q8.24} slopes and $\Sigma\Delta$ remainder diffusion matches IEEE-754 \texttt{FP64} within a maximum relative error of $0.0563\%$ (terminal error $\le 0.0296\%$), while the bare-metal C99 mixed \texttt{Q16.16}/\texttt{Q8.24} 7-state Huber-EKF tracks the perturbed 8-state plant under $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ noise and $5\%$ $6.5\sigma$ spikes with $0.94\%$ heteroplasmy RMSE (switching at step $415$, $37.35\text{ d}$, and restoring $W(T_{\max}) = 1.0518, h(T_{\max}) = 0.0222$). Sweeping $N \in \{500, 1{,}000, 2{,}000, 4{,}000, 8{,}000\}$ ($\Delta t \in [0.180, 0.01125]\text{ d}$) demonstrates why $\Sigma\Delta$ remainder diffusion is essential for integer SIMD: naive round-to-nearest \texttt{Q16.16} \texttt{RK4} diverges from $13.51\%$ error at $N=500$ to $100.00\%$ deadband stall at $N=8{,}000$, and \texttt{Q8.24} slopes without remainder still degrade to $17.44\%\text{--}22.02\%$ at $N \ge 4{,}000$, whereas \texttt{Q8.24} slopes with 8-lane $\Sigma\Delta$ remainder diffusion maintain $0.0534\%\text{--}0.0563\%$ error across the entire $16\times$ step-refinement range (compared with $0.0001\%\text{--}0.0022\%$ for single-lane scalar \texttt{RV32F} \texttt{FP32}). Across $2{,}560$ full ODE integrations, Saltelli Sobol indices on $\log_{10}(1 + J(\mathbf{u}))$ confirm that within the viable physiological box ($\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} \in [1.02, 1.25]$), Stage-1 mitophagy capacity dominates variance ($S_i = 0.739, S_{T_i} = 0.859$), followed by $k_{\mathrm{clr}}$ ($0.125/0.125$) and $k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}$ ($0.102/0.138$), whereas dropping $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ below $\mathrm{EP}(0)$ into $[0.92, 1.02]$ triggers a sharp trans-cliff collapse transition ($S_{T_i}(\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}) = 0.989$).

Table~\ref{tab:memory_power} details both the compiler-verified single-bank L1 DTCM layout (\texttt{sizeof(coralnpu\_dtcm\_bank0\_workspace\_t)} $= 3{,}696\text{ B}$ out of $8{,}192\text{ B}$ in \texttt{Bank~0}, leaving $4{,}496\text{ B}$ Bank~0 headroom and $24\text{ KB}$ in \texttt{Banks~1--3} power-gated) and the host-emulated cycle-weighted power estimate at $500\text{ MHz}$ ($22\text{ nm}$ ULP CMOS model): sequential execution over $3{,}006$ cycles ($6.012\;\mu\text{s}$) yields a cycle-weighted mean active NPU burst power of $3.090\text{ mW}$ ($18.58\text{ nJ}$/burst), accounted separately from external analog front-end sensor excitation ($1.800\text{ mW}$ during its $15\text{ ms}$ ADC window).

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Reproducibility Manifest}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Limitations and Scope of Claims.}
First, all ODE trajectories, unconstrained optimal control solutions, mismatched EKF benchmarks, and C99 fixed-point results are computational and host-emulated \textit{in silico} evaluations; no prospective wet-lab, animal, or clinical claims are made. Second, as Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}B and Table~\ref{tab:ekf_ablation}A establish, biological controllability and indirect observability have hard physical limits: initial wild-type depletion inside Zone~0 ($18.9\%$ of the $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ grid) is irreversibly unrescuable without exogenous organelle transplantation, and four-channel proxy sensing leaves pre-switch $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$ ill-conditioning ($\sim 1.9\%$ heteroplasmy RMSE under $\pm 20\%$ kinetic mismatch) requiring conservative threshold margin tightening ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.065$). Third, discrete-copy stochastic drift at ultra-low mtDNA copy number, spatially resolved axonal fission-fusion reaction-diffusion PDEs, and gate-level RTL power synthesis on physical FPGA or Coral NPU silicon lie outside the deterministic single-compartment scope of this paper.

\paragraph{MIASE Reproducibility Manifest.}
Following Minimum Information About a Simulation Experiment (MIASE) guidelines \cite{waltemath2011miase}, every figure, table entry, and numerical claim in this manuscript is deterministically generated from two zero-stub executable artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{ODE, Adjoint Optimal Control, Mismatched EKF, Phase-Plane \& Sobol Suite:} Run \texttt{python3 simulator/\allowbreak generate\_\allowbreak paper1\_\allowbreak verified\_\allowbreak benchmarks.py} (fixed PRNG seed \texttt{20261109}), which integrates all three non-homothetic 8-state ODE regimes, solves the 5-start 300-variable discrete-adjoint \texttt{L-BFGS-B} optimal control problem, computes the Stage-1 7-state Observability Gramian and 1,000-step 120-trial $\pm 20\%$ mismatched closed-loop Huber-EKF benchmark, maps the $31 \times 31$ $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ phase plane, runs the $1{,}000$-stream $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ persistence and $2{,}560$-run Saltelli Sobol audits, compiles/parses the C99 testbench, writes \texttt{simulator/\allowbreak paper1\_\allowbreak verified\_\allowbreak benchmarks.json}, and renders Figures~\ref{fig:ode_control}--\ref{fig:hardware_verification}.
  \item \textbf{Bare-Metal C99 \texttt{Q16.16} \texttt{RK4}, Sequential Huber-EKF \& Deadband Ablation Testbench:} Compile and run \texttt{cc -std=c99 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror build/coralnpu/coralnpu\_hil\_testbench.c -lm -o build/coralnpu/coralnpu\_hil\_testbench \&\& ./build/coralnpu/coralnpu\_hil\_testbench}, verifying 1,000-step \texttt{Q16.16} parity (\texttt{MaxErr = 0.0563\%}), 256-trial 8-lane \texttt{Zve32x} widening parity (\texttt{MaxULPDiff = 0}), closed-loop C99 Huber-EKF (\texttt{h\_RMSE = 0.94\%}, \texttt{sizeof = 3696B/8192B}), and the 5-step-size quantization deadband ablation.
