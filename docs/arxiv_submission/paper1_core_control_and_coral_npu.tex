\begin{abstract}
\noindent Restoring bioenergetics in cells with mutant mitochondria ($D$) alongside wild-type organelles ($W$) requires navigating nonlinear couplings among clearance bottlenecks ($B$), reactive oxygen species (ROS) conversion, and replicative selection ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} \ge 1$). We formulate an eight-state ODE model unified 1-to-1 across analytical equations, FP64 simulation, and bare-metal C99 firmware to analyze staged feedback control: bottleneck clearance ($u_0$), selective mitophagy ($u_1$), and biogenesis ($u_2$) gated at heteroplasmy $h \le 0.080$ and $B \le 0.120$. Via Nagumo's subtangent theorem, we prove forward invariance of the Stage-1 safe set ($\mathrm{EP} \le 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$, $W_{\min} = 0.600 > 0.450$, $\lambda_{\min} = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1}$), derive exact selection collapse $s(t) = \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq$ ($0.0000$ error), and bound constant-rate rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$ across three non-homothetic regimes ($R^2 = 0.9828$, sup error $0.0493 \le 0.1213$). Discrete-adjoint optimal control (300 variables, gradient error $1.95 \times 10^{-9}$) shows Pontryagin trajectories ride $\mathrm{EP} = \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ ($J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$ vs.\ threshold $J_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 0.8875$), trading $+22.21\%$ cost for a $20.1\%$ redox margin, while a $31 \times 31$ $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ map separates collapse ($18.9\%$) from build-first rescue ($2.9\%$). Under 4-channel sensing ($\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$), $\pm 20\%$ rate mismatch, and $6.5\sigma$ spikes, margin-tightened Huber-EKF ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.065$) cuts premature switches from $24.17\%$ to $5.00\%$. On host-emulated Coral NPU (\texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x}, 3,696 B DTCM), fixed-point Huber-EKF ($0.94\%$ RMSE) and Q16.16/Q8.24 $\Sigma\Delta$ RK4 prevent deadband stalling ($0.0534\%$--$0.0563\%$ error vs.\ $100\%$ naive stall).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Nonlinear Coupling in Mitochondrial Quality Control}
Eukaryotic bioenergetics depends on maintaining a high functional fraction of intact wild-type mitochondrial DNA and cristae ($W$) relative to deletion-bearing or depolarized mutant organelles ($D$) \cite{wallace2012mitochondrial,stewart2015purifying,aryaman2019stochastic}. Across diverse biological timescales---including post-mitotic dopaminergic neurons in Parkinsonian neurodegeneration ($T_{\max} = 90\text{ d}$) \cite{surmeier2017selective,bolam2012living}, \textit{ex vivo} chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell expansion bioreactors ($T_{\max} = 168\text{ h}$) \cite{fraietta2018determinants,sukumar2013inhibiting,buck2016mitochondrial}, and honeybee (\textit{Apis mellifera}) thoracic flight muscle exposed to strobilurin Complex~III inhibition ($T_{\max} = 14\text{ d}$) \cite{degrandi2015fungicides,seehuus2006bee}---mitochondrial decline is governed by three interacting feedbacks:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Upstream Clearance Bottlenecks ($B$):} Accumulation of $\alpha$-synuclein oligomers, lipofuscin, or xenobiotic fungicides elevates energetic impedance and saturates autophagosome-lysosome competence $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) = K_B / (K_B + B) \in (0, 1]$ \cite{surmeier2017selective,ryu2016urolithin}.
  \item \textbf{Threshold ROS Conversion ($\mathrm{EP} > \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$):} When cellular energetic potential $\mathrm{EP}$ exceeds the glutathione/peroxiredoxin antioxidant buffering threshold $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$, electron leak generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) that convert intact wild-type mass $W$ into damaged mass $D$ \cite{picard2025energy}.
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric Replicative Selection ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}$):} Deletion-bearing mtDNA templates or fragmented organelles frequently exhibit a replicative or survival advantage ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} \ge 1$) during non-selective biogenesis \cite{stewart2015purifying,kowald2014mtDNA}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with Prior Control Formulations}
In a foundational mathematical study, Hoitzing et al.~\cite{hoitzing2019energetic} analyzed stochastic and deterministic mtDNA population control in a two-species $(W, D)$ system without an upstream lysosomal bottleneck state $B(t)$ or replicative advantage ($k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} = 1$). When wild-type mass $W_0$ is already near the minimum survival floor and $B_0 \approx 0$, Hoitzing et al.\ demonstrated that an initial biogenesis pulse (``build-first'') prevents transient ATP starvation before selective clearance. Conversely, when an upstream bottleneck $B_0 > 0$ elevates bioenergetic impedance and $k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} > 1$, stimulating biogenesis ($u_2 > 0$) prior to clearing $B$ and $D$ drives the energy potential $\mathrm{EP}(t)$ above $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ and expands the mutant pool. Defining the exact mathematical boundary between these regimes---and implementing a verifiable closed-loop controller within the strict memory and arithmetic constraints of an ultra-low-power neural processing unit (NPU)---remains an open systems-and-control problem.

\subsection{Contributions and Manuscript Organization}
This study is a strictly theoretical and host-emulated computational investigation. All complete 8-state differential equations, auxiliary algebraic rate definitions, and step-by-step mathematical proofs are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:math} so that the main text focuses clearly on the governing dynamics and control results:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Core Organelle Balance and Joint Safety Invariance (\S2.1--\S2.2, Appendices~\ref{app:model}--\ref{app:invariance}):} We formulate an 8-state bioenergetic plant unified 1-to-1 across \LaTeX, Python (\path{eval_rhs_fp64}), and C99 (\path{coralnpu_eval_core8_rhs_q16}), prove joint forward invariance of the Stage-1 safe box via Nagumo's subtangent theorem ($\mathrm{EP} \le 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$, $W_{\min} = 0.600 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$, Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}), and establish regional exponential contraction ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1}$, Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}).
  \item \textbf{Discrete-Adjoint Optimal Control and 2D Controllability Phase Plane (\S2.3--\S2.4):} Solving a 300-variable discrete-adjoint optimal control problem across 5 initializations ($0.12\%$ cost spread) decomposes the optimality gap among Pontryagin interior-arc control ($J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$), optimal two-stage constant relay ($J_{\mathrm{2stage}}^* = 0.850$), and closed-loop threshold feedback ($J_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 0.8875$), while a $31 \times 31$ $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ phase diagram maps irreversible collapse ($18.9\%$) vs.\ build-first rescue ($2.9\%$).
  \item \textbf{Accumulated-Selection Reduction Across Timescales (\S3, Appendix~\ref{app:reduction}):} We separate exact accumulated-selection rescaling $s(t) = \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq$ ($0.0000$ error) from zero-tuning constant-rate rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$ across three non-homothetic regimes, verifying the analytical error bound in every regime (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, Table~\ref{tab:collapse}).
  \item \textbf{Mismatched Huber-EKF Observability and \path{RV32IMF_Zve32x} Firmware (\S4--\S5, Appendix~\ref{app:persistence}):} We audit pre-switch 7-state Observability Gramian conditioning ($\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$), benchmark Python and C99 fixed-point Huber-EKF under $\pm 20\%$ kinetic mismatch and $6.5\sigma$ spikes, prove a reversible Markov persistence bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence}) with continuous-plant actuator rollback ($\Delta h_{\max} = +0.0098$), and verify single-bank L1 DTCM residency ($3{,}696\text{ B}$) and deadband-free $\Sigma\Delta$ \texttt{RK4} integration ($0.0534\%\text{--}0.0563\%$ error).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Organelle Population Balance and Indirect Observability}
We model the intracellular mitochondrial network over $t \in [0, T_{\max}]$ via an eight-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{x}(t) = [W,\, D,\, B,\, E,\, G,\, N,\, C,\, M_8]^\top \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^8$, actuated by bounded controls $\mathbf{u}(t) = [u_0(t), u_1(t), u_2(t)]^\top \in [0, 1]^3$ representing upstream bottleneck clearance ($u_0$), PINK1/Parkin selective mitophagy ($u_1$), and PGC-1$\alpha$/TFAM mitochondrial biogenesis ($u_2$). Let $M(t) = W(t) + D(t)$ denote total organelle mass and $h(t) = D(t)/M(t) \in [0, 1)$ denote the mutant heteroplasmy fraction.

While all eight coupled differential equations and closed-form auxiliary rates ($\mathrm{ATP}$, impedance $\dot{e}_R$, effective load $f_{\mathrm{eff}}$, energy potential $\mathrm{EP}$, and cofactor states $E, G, N, C, M_8$) are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:model} and parameterized in Table~\ref{tab:params}, the core population selection dynamics are governed by the $(W, D, B)$ subsystem:
\begin{align}
  \dot{W} &= k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\,W\!\left(1 - \frac{M}{K}\right) - \Big(k_{\mathrm{mito},W} + k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max\!\big(0,\, \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)\Big)W, \label{eq:ode_W} \\
  \dot{D} &= k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\,D\!\left(1 - \frac{M}{K}\right) - k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B)\,D + k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max\!\big(0,\, \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)W, \label{eq:ode_D} \\
  \dot{B} &= b_{\mathrm{basal}} + k_{\mathrm{acc}}\,D\,(1 - u_0) - k_{\mathrm{clr}}\,u_0\,B. \label{eq:ode_B}
\end{align}
Here $K > 1$ is the carrying capacity, $k_{\mathrm{bio},0} = k_{\mathrm{mito},W}/(1 - 1/K)$ calibrates the unstimulated homeostatic equilibrium to $(W^*, D^*) = (1.00, 0)$ ($k_{\mathrm{bio},0} = 0.075\text{ d}^{-1}$ for $K = 1.250, k_{\mathrm{mito},W} = 0.015\text{ d}^{-1}$), and $\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) = \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x})(1 - c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2)^2$ couples proteostatic bottleneck load $B$ and heteroplasmy $h$ to supra-threshold ROS damage above $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$.

Because $(W, D, B)$ are not directly measurable at embedded sampling rates, the controller observes a discrete-time four-channel proxy $\mathbf{y}_k = [E(t_k),\, \mathrm{ATP}(\mathbf{x}(t_k)),\, G(t_k),\, N(t_k)]^\top + \mathbf{v}_k$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:observation_model} in Appendix~\ref{app:model}). At any single time instant $t_k$, the product $\mathrm{EP}\cdot\mathrm{ATP}/f_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 = 1 + \beta_B B + \beta_h h$ depends on $(B, h)$ only through a single linear combination, while $(W, D)$ and phosphagen buffer $C$ exhibit multiplicative iso-ATP trade-offs. Reconstructing $(\hat{W}, \hat{D}, \hat{B})$ therefore requires dynamic multi-step state estimation via a 7-state Huber-regularized extended Kalman filter (Huber-EKF) \cite{kalman1960new,huber1964robust}, evaluated under $\pm 20\%$ plant-model mismatch in \S5.2.

\subsection{Heteroplasmy Quotient Flow and Stage-1 Safety Invariance}
Differentiating $h(t) = D(t)/[W(t)+D(t)]$ along Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_D} (full quotient-rule algebra in Appendix~\ref{app:invariance}) yields the exact heteroplasmy evolution law:
\begin{align}
  \dot{h}(t) &= -\lambda_h(t)\,h(t)\big(1 - h(t)\big) + \big(1 - h(t)\big)\,k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max\!\big(0,\, \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big), \label{eq:h_dot} \\
  \lambda_h(t) &= k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)\,V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)\,k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N)\!\left(1 - \frac{M(t)}{K}\right). \label{eq:lambda_h}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:h_dot}--\eqref{eq:lambda_h} expose the core dynamical conflict: when $k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} > 1$ and $B(t)$ is elevated, activating biogenesis prematurely ($u_2 = 1$) subtracts $(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)k_{\mathrm{bio}}(1 - M/K)$ from the selective clearance rate $\lambda_h(t)$ while simultaneously raising $f_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and pushing $\mathrm{EP}$ above $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$, igniting positive ROS conversion of $W$ into $D$. Conversely, Stage~1 clearance ($u_0 = 1, u_1 = 1, u_2 = 0$) unblocks $B(t)$ and lowers $f_{\mathrm{eff}}$, preserving joint forward invariance of a safe physiological box:

\begin{lemma}[Joint Stage-1 Safe-Box Invariance; Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:invariance}]
\label{lem:invariance}
Under Stage~1 control ($u_0 = 1, u_1 = 1, u_2 = 0$) on the compact candidate box $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0) = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^8 : W \in [W^* - D_0, K],\, D \in [0, D_0],\, B \in [B_{\mathrm{eq}}, B_0],\, E \in [0, E_{\max}],\, C \in [C_{\min}, 1.30]\}$ with $\sup_{\Omega_1} \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, (1,1,0)) \le \min(\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}, E_{\max})$ and critical bottleneck threshold $B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:B_crit}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Joint Forward Invariance ($B_0 \le B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$):} $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ is positively invariant; throughout $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, ROS conversion remains identically zero ($\mathrm{EP} \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$), $D(t) \le D_0$, and wild-type mass never drops below $W_{\min}(D_0) = W^* - D_0$. On the canonical neuronal box ($D_0 = 0.400, B_0 = 0.500 < B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* = 1.2020$), $\max_{\Omega_1}\mathrm{EP} = 1.0456 < \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$ and $W(t) \ge 0.600 > \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} = 0.450$.
  \item \textbf{Super-Critical Dwell Time ($B_0 > B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$):} Under $u_0 = 1$, $B(t)$ decays exponentially to $B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$ in finite time $t_0 = k_{\mathrm{clr}}^{-1}\ln[(B_0 - B_{\mathrm{eq}})/(B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* - B_{\mathrm{eq}})]$, after which Case~1 invariance holds whenever $\bar{D}(t_0) < W^*$ ($t_0 = 2.91\text{ d}$ at $B_0 = 2.00$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Regional Contraction and Invasion Stability; Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:invariance}]
\label{prop:stability}
For any initial state $\mathbf{x}(0) \in \Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$ satisfying Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} Case~1, the selective contraction rate $\lambda_h(t)$ is bounded below uniformly on $[0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$ by a closed-form constant $\lambda_{\min}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:lambda_min_analytic}; $\lambda_{\min} = 0.01085\text{ d}^{-1} > 0$ on the canonical neuronal box). Whenever $\lambda_{\min} > 0$, heteroplasmy contracts monotonically according to
\begin{equation}
  h(t) \le \frac{h_0\,e^{-\lambda_{\min} t}}{1 - h_0 + h_0\,e^{-\lambda_{\min} t}} \le h_0\exp\!\big(-\lambda_{\min}(1 - h_0)t\big).
  \label{eq:prop1_bounds}
\end{equation}
Moreover, at the mutant-free homeostatic equilibrium $(W^*, 0, B_{\mathrm{eq}})$, the linearized mutant invasion eigenvalue $\mu_D = k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1)V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}})$ switches sign from unstable ($\mu_D > 0$ when unstimulated) to locally exponentially stable ($\mu_D < 0$) under Stage-1 mitophagy.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Discrete-Adjoint Optimal Control vs.\ Embedded Threshold Feedback}
To benchmark simple threshold switching against an unconstrained time-varying control schedule identically to \texttt{compute\_bolza\_cost} in \path{simulator/generate_paper1_verified_benchmarks.py}, we minimize the finite-horizon Bolza cost functional:
\begin{align}
  J(\mathbf{u}) &= w_h\,h(T_{\max})^2 + w_W\big(W_{\mathrm{ref}} - W(T_{\max})\big)^2 + \int_0^{T_{\max}} \Big[ w_{\mathrm{EP}}\max\!\big(0, \mathrm{EP}(t) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)^2 \nonumber\\
  &\quad + w_{\mathrm{surv}}\max\!\big(0, \theta_{\mathrm{surv}} - \mathrm{ATP}(t)\big)^2 + \frac{w_{\mathrm{def}}}{T_{\max}}\max\!\big(0, 0.85 - W(t)\big)^2 + r_1 u_1(t) + r_2 u_2(t) \Big]\,dt,
  \label{eq:bolza_cost}
\end{align}
with terminal weights $w_h = 80, w_W = 30$ ($W_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1.00$), safety weights $w_{\mathrm{EP}} = 180\text{ d}^{-1}, w_{\mathrm{surv}} = 250\text{ d}^{-1}, w_{\mathrm{def}} = 0.85$, and horizon-normalized actuation weights $r_1 = 1.15/T_{\max}, r_2 = 0.12/T_{\max}$ ($r_0 = 0 \implies u_0^*(t) \equiv 1$).

Discretizing $\mathbf{u}(t) \in [0, 1]^3$ into $N_{\mathrm{ctrl}} = 100$ piecewise-constant intervals ($300$ decision variables over $1{,}000$ exact \texttt{RK4} steps on $[0, 90\text{ d}]$) and propagating exact discrete-time \texttt{RK4} adjoint Jacobians (central finite-difference relative error $1.95 \times 10^{-9}$), \texttt{L-BFGS-B} \cite{byrd1995limited,virtanen2020scipy} converges across 5 diverse initializations within a $0.12\%$ cost spread ($J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^* = 0.7262$, $h(T_{\max}) = 0.0300, W(T_{\max}) = 0.9938$; Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}A). Rather than pure bang-bang switching, $u_1^*(t)$ modulates smoothly along an early interior redox arc ($[0.44, 0.83]$ on Days~$0\text{--}17$) so $\mathrm{EP}^*(t)$ rides $\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}} = 1.150$, while $u_2^*(t)$ remains zero until $h^*(t) < 0.030$ before executing a terminal biogenesis sprint (Days~$78\text{--}90$).

Because riding $\mathrm{EP}^*(t) \equiv \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ in open loop leaves zero robustness margin against kinetic mismatch, our embedded closed-loop law triggers Stage~1 $\to$ Stage~2 ($(1, 1, 0) \to (1, 0.35, 1)$) when $B(t) \le B_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.120$ and $h(t) \le h_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:hgate_derivation} in Appendix~\ref{app:model}). At $h_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.080$ ($t_{\mathrm{gate}} = 33.30\text{ d}$), post-switch $\mathrm{EP}(t_{\mathrm{gate}}^+)$ steps from $0.603$ to $0.919 < 1.150$, preserving a $20.1\%$ redox margin while incurring a decomposed $+17.05\%$ relay-restriction cost ($J_{\mathrm{2stage}}^* = 0.850$) plus a $+4.38\%$ constant-threshold cost ($J_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 0.8875$; $+22.21\%$ total vs.\ $J_{\mathrm{PMP}}^*$, Figure~\ref{fig:ode_control}B).

\section{Accumulated-Selection Reduction Across Biological Timescales}
% =============================================================================

Dimensional analysis alone \cite{buckingham1914physically} cannot collapse an 8-state nonlinear ODE onto a 1D curve unless the time-variation of $\lambda_h(t)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} is explicitly accounted for. We therefore establish both the exact accumulated-selection identity and a zero-tuning closed-form perturbation bound for constant-rate time rescaling.

\begin{proposition}[Accumulated-Selection Reduction; Proof \& Closed-Form Integral in Appendix~\ref{app:reduction}]
\label{prop:reduction}
Suppose $\mathrm{EP}(t) \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}$ on $[0, t]$ during Stage~1 ($u_0=1, u_1=1, u_2=0$) with $h_0 \in (0, 1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact Accumulated-Selection Coordinate:} In terms of the dimensionless selection integral $s(t) \triangleq \int_0^t \lambda_h(q)\,dq$, the 8-state heteroplasmy trajectory satisfies the exact logistic law
  \begin{equation}
    h(t) = h_{\mathrm{logistic}}\big(s(t); h_0\big) \triangleq \frac{h_0\,e^{-s(t)}}{1 - h_0 + h_0\,e^{-s(t)}}, \qquad s^*(h_0, h_{\mathrm{gate}}) = \ln\!\left(\frac{h_0(1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}})}{h_{\mathrm{gate}}(1 - h_0)}\right),
    \label{eq:h_logistic_exact}
  \end{equation}
  yielding $s^*(0.40, 0.08) = 2.0369$, $s^*(0.25, 0.08) = 1.3437$, and $s^*(0.60, 0.08) = 2.8478$.
  \item \textbf{Zero-Tuning Perturbation Bound for Constant-Rate Rescaling $\hat{\tau} = \hat{\lambda}_0 t$:} Let $\hat{\lambda}_0$ denote the closed-form \textit{a priori} Stage-1 contraction rate obtained by integrating $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(q))$ analytically over the cleared-bottleneck horizon estimate (Eqs.~\ref{eq:vlys_bar_closed}--\ref{eq:lambda_hat0} in Appendix~\ref{app:reduction}, with zero fitted constants). Then for all $t \in [0, t_{\mathrm{gate}}]$, the constant-rate approximation error satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \big|h(t) - h_{\mathrm{logistic}}(\hat{\lambda}_0 t; h_0)\big| \le \frac{1}{4}\Big(\mathcal{E}_B(t) + \mathcal{E}_M(t)\Big),
    \label{eq:sup_err_bound}
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathcal{E}_B(t)$ and $\mathcal{E}_M(t)$ bound transient bottleneck-clearance and total-mass variations (Eq.~\ref{eq:multi_term_bound}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

We target the open-source Google Coral NPU architecture (\texttt{RV32IMF\_Zve32x}) \cite{coralnpu2025datasheet,waterman2014riscv}, pairing a 32-bit RISC-V scalar core (with scalar single-precision \texttt{FP32}) and an integer-only 128-bit SIMD vector unit (\texttt{Zve32x}, four 32-bit or eight 16-bit integer lanes) with $8\text{ KB}$ ITCM and $32\text{ KB}$ DTCM split into four $8{,}192\text{-byte}$ banks. Constraining the entire runtime state (INT8 gate/Goertzel LUTs, mixed \texttt{Q16.16}/\texttt{Q8.24} 7-state Huber-EKF covariance matrices, \texttt{RK4} slope registers, 64-frame ring buffers, and stack) to \path{coralnpu_dtcm_bank0_workspace_t} occupies \textbf{$3{,}696\text{ B}$ ($45.12\%$) of a single $8{,}192\text{-byte}$ bank (\texttt{Bank~0})}, allowing \texttt{Banks~1}--\texttt{3} ($24\text{ KB}$) to remain power-gated during chronic monitoring (Figure~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}D, Table~\ref{tab:memory_power}).

\subsection{Widening Vector \texttt{RK4}, $\Sigma\Delta$ Remainder Diffusion, and Integer Huber-EKF}
In \path{build/coralnpu/mitorestorer_coralnpu_kernel.h}, exact integer firmware parity across multi-week horizons combines three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{0-ULP 64-Bit Widening Vector Updates:} Multiplying \texttt{Q16.16} operands or scaling \texttt{Q8.24} slopes by $\Delta t_{\mathrm{Q16}}$ uses 32-to-64-bit widening SIMD multiplication (\path{__riscv_vwmul_vx_i64m4}) and narrowing arithmetic shift (\path{__riscv_vnsra_wx_i32m2}), achieving \textbf{0-ULP discrepancy} against scalar 64-bit integer reference across $2{,}048$ randomized lane operations.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Q8.24} Slopes with 8-Lane $\Sigma\Delta$ Remainder Diffusion:} Slow biological turnover ($k_{\mathrm{mito},W} = 0.015\text{ d}^{-1}$) at small step sizes $\Delta t \le 0.045\text{ d}$ produces per-step increments smaller than $0.5\text{ LSB}_{16}$ ($7.63 \times 10^{-6}$), stalling naive \texttt{Q16.16} \texttt{RK4} completely (Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_ekf_phase}D). Evaluating derivatives in \texttt{Q8.24} ($2^{-24} \approx 5.96 \times 10^{-8}$) and propagating the exact 64-bit numerator remainder $\mathbf{r}_k \in (-6\cdot 2^{24}, 6\cdot 2^{24})^8$ across steps eliminates quantization deadband drift across a $16\times$ step sweep.
  \item \textbf{Zero-Heap Mixed \texttt{Q16.16}/\texttt{Q8.24} Sequential Huber-EKF:} Without \texttt{malloc} or \texttt{<math.h>}, \path{coralnpu_huber_ekf7_meas_update_q16} stores $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0:6}$ in \texttt{Q16.16} and $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{Z}_{32}^{7\times 7}$ in \texttt{Q8.24}, processes the four proxy channels sequentially via scalar innovation variances $S_i = \mathbf{h}_i\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}_i^\top + R_i$, computes $\sqrt{S_i}$ via exact 64-bit integer Newton square root (\path{coralnpu_isqrt64}), and applies symmetric Huber clipping $w_{\mathrm{Hub}} = \min(1, \gamma_{\mathrm{Huber}}\sqrt{S_i}/|\nu_i|)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{64-Frame Dual-Modality Persistence Under Markov Noise}
To prevent correlated transient spikes from triggering premature switching, a 64-frame bitmask $\mathcal{B}_k \in \{0,1\}^{64}$ requires $M_{\mathrm{req}} = 60$ primary exceedances ($\mu_{\mathrm{req}} = 60/64 = 0.9375$) plus a concurrent secondary modality check $z_k \ge \gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Markov Persistence Bound; Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:persistence}]
\label{prop:persistence}
Let stationary sensor noise evolve via the reversible Gaussian $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ Markov chain $e_k = \rho e_{k-1} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\epsilon_k$ ($\rho \in [0, 1)$, spectral gap $1 - \rho$), with primary exceedance probability $p_y < \mu_{\mathrm{req}} = 0.9375$, quorum-conditioned secondary probability $p_{z|Q}$, and $c_0 \in \{0,\dots,64\}$ prior buffer exceedances before a contiguous burst of $L_{\mathrm{dist}}$ frames:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic Sub-Quorum Cutoff:} False quorum is deterministically impossible whenever $L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59 - c_0$ ($L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59$ under a clean prior buffer $c_0 = 0$; Figure~\ref{fig:hardware_verification}B).
  \item \textbf{Spectral-Gap False-Trigger Bound:} By Paulin's concentration inequality for reversible Markov chains \cite{paulin2015concentration}, single-step and $K_{\mathrm{frames}}$-horizon false-trigger probabilities satisfy
  \begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{FP,step}} \le p_{z|Q}\exp\!\left(-2 N_{\mathrm{win}}\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}(\mu_{\mathrm{req}} - p_y)^2\right), \qquad P_{\mathrm{FP,horizon}} \le \min\!\big(1,\, K_{\mathrm{frames}} P_{\mathrm{FP,step}}\big).
    \label{eq:paulin_step}
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

In physical deployments, the 64-frame window $T_{\mathrm{win}} = 64\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{frame}}$ scales with plant kinetics: $\Delta t_{\mathrm{frame}} = 1.0\text{ s}$ for \textit{Apis} wingbeat telemetry ($T_{\mathrm{win}} = 64\text{ s}$), $1.0\text{ min}$ for CAR-T bioreactors ($64\text{ min}$), and $15.0\text{ min}$ for chronic neural monitoring ($16.0\text{ h}$), verified across $1{,}000$ correlated $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ streams in Table~\ref{tab:policy_comparison}B.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Reproducibility Manifest}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Limitations and Scope of Claims.}
All ODE trajectories, discrete-adjoint optimal control schedules, mismatched EKF benchmarks, and C99 fixed-point results in this study are strictly computational and host-emulated \textit{in silico} evaluations; no prospective wet-lab, animal, or clinical claims are made. Biological controllability and indirect observability exhibit hard physical limits: initial wild-type depletion inside Zone~0 ($18.9\%$ of the $(W_0, k_{\mathrm{dmg}})$ grid) is irreversibly unrescuable without exogenous organelle transfer, and 4-channel proxy sensing leaves $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_7) = 745.6$ pre-switch ill-conditioning requiring conservative threshold margin tightening ($\hat{h}_{\mathrm{gate}} = 0.065$). Stochastic copy-number drift at very low mtDNA counts, spatial axonal reaction-diffusion PDEs, and physical RTL silicon synthesis lie outside the deterministic single-compartment scope of Paper~I.

\subsection{Complete 8-State System, Auxiliary Rates, and Observation Map}
\label{app:model}
To guarantee exact 1-to-1 parity between \LaTeX, \path{eval_rhs_fp64} in Python, and \path{coralnpu_eval_core8_rhs_q16} in C99, the closed-form auxiliary bioenergetic rates are:
\begin{align}
  V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B) &= \frac{K_B}{K_B + B}, \qquad
  k_{\mathrm{bio}}(u_2, N) = k_{\mathrm{bio},0} + (k_{\mathrm{bio},\max} - k_{\mathrm{bio},0})\,u_2\,\frac{N}{1 + N}, \label{eq:aux_kbio} \\
  k_{\mathrm{mito},D}(u_1) &= k_{\mathrm{mito},W} + (k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W})\,u_1, \qquad
  \mathrm{ATP}(\mathbf{x}) = (W + \eta_D D)(1 + \gamma_C C), \label{eq:aux_atp} \\
  \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1 + \beta_B B + \beta_h h}{\max(0.10,\, W + \eta_D D)(1 + \gamma_C C)}, \qquad
  f_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_1, u_2) = 1 - c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2, \label{eq:aux_er} \\
  \mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) &= \dot{e}_R(\mathbf{x})\,f_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_1, u_2)^2, \qquad
  k_{\mathrm{bio},0} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},W}}{1 - 1/K}. \label{eq:aux_ep}
\end{align}
Together with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_B} for $(W, D, B)$, the remaining five state equations for filtered energy potential $E$, secreted stress marker $G$, redox cofactor $N$, phosphagen buffer $C$, and epigenetic drift readout $M_8$ are:
\begin{align}
  \dot{E} &= k_E\big(\mathrm{EP}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) - E\big), \qquad
  \dot{G} = k_{G,\mathrm{prod}}\,E - k_{G,\mathrm{clr}}\,G, \label{eq:ode_EG} \\
  \dot{N} &= k_{N,\mathrm{syn},0} + k_{N,\mathrm{syn},2}\,u_2 - k_{\mathrm{PARP}}\max(0, E - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})N - k_{N,\mathrm{use}}\,N, \label{eq:ode_N} \\
  \dot{C} &= (k_{\mathrm{oct},0} + k_{\mathrm{oct},2}\,u_2)(1.30 - C) - k_{\mathrm{ox}}\max(0, E - \theta_{\mathrm{cys}})C, \label{eq:ode_C} \\
  \dot{M}_8 &= k_{\mathrm{drift}}\,E\,(10.0 - M_8) - k_{\mathrm{TET}}\,u_2\,N\,C\,M_8. \label{eq:ode_M8}
\end{align}
At discrete controller steps $t_k$, the 4-channel proxy observation vector and post-switch redox safety condition ($u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} = 0.35$) are:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{y}_k &= \big[E(t_k),\, \mathrm{ATP}(\mathbf{x}(t_k)),\, G(t_k),\, N(t_k)\big]^\top + \mathbf{v}_k, \label{eq:observation_model} \\
  \mathrm{EP}(t_{\mathrm{gate}}^+) &= \frac{1 + \beta_B B(t_{\mathrm{gate}}) + \beta_h h_{\mathrm{gate}}}{W(t_{\mathrm{gate}})\big[1 + \eta_D h_{\mathrm{gate}}/(1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}})\big]\big[1 + \gamma_C C(t_{\mathrm{gate}})\big]}\,(1 - c_1 u_{1,\mathrm{S2}} + c_2)^2 \le \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}. \label{eq:hgate_derivation}
\end{align}

\subsection{Quotient-Rule Derivation and Proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} and Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}}
\label{app:invariance}
Applying the quotient rule $\dot{h} = (\dot{D}W - D\dot{W})/M^2$ to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ode_W}--\eqref{eq:ode_D}, let $d_{\mathrm{ros}} \triangleq k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\max(0, \mathrm{EP} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})$. The $+d_{\mathrm{ros}}W$ source term in $\dot{D}$ contributes $(W^2/M^2)d_{\mathrm{ros}} = (1 - h)^2 d_{\mathrm{ros}}$, while the $-d_{\mathrm{ros}}W$ loss term in $\dot{W}$ contributes $+(DW/M^2)d_{\mathrm{ros}} = h(1 - h)d_{\mathrm{ros}}$; their sum is $(1 - h)d_{\mathrm{ros}}$, yielding Eqs.~\eqref{eq:h_dot}--\eqref{eq:lambda_h}.

Define the boundary and interior critical bottleneck thresholds:
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* \triangleq K_B\!\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}}{k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\,(1 - W^*/K)} - 1\right), \qquad
  B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}} \triangleq K_B\!\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}}{k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}\,k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\,\big[1 - (W^* - D_0)/K\big]} - 1\right).
  \label{eq:B_crit}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}]
Under $(u_0, u_1, u_2) = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\sup_{\Omega_1}\mathrm{EP} \le \min(\theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}, E_{\max})$, $d_{\mathrm{ros}} \equiv 0$ on $\Omega_1$. Checking Nagumo's subtangent condition on each face of $\Omega_1$: $\dot{E}|_{E=0} \ge 0$, $\dot{E}|_{E=E_{\max}} \le 0$, $\dot{C}|_{C=1.30} \le 0$, $\dot{C}|_{C=C_{\min}} \ge 0$ (when $k_{\mathrm{oct},0}(1.30 - C_{\min}) \ge k_{\mathrm{ox}}\max(0, E_{\max} - \theta_{\mathrm{cys}})C_{\min}$), $\dot{B}|_{B=B_{\mathrm{eq}}} = 0$, and $\dot{B}|_{B=B_0} = k_{\mathrm{clr}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}} - B_0) \le 0$. On the upper mutant face $D = D_0$, $W \ge W^* - D_0 \implies M = W + D_0 \ge W^*$, so $\dot{D}|_{D=D_0} \le D_0[k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}k_{\mathrm{bio},0}(1 - W^*/K) - k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}K_B/(K_B + B)] \le 0$ for all $B \le B_{\mathrm{crit}}^*$. On the lower wild-type face $W = W_{\min} = W^* - D_0$, $D \le D_0 \implies M \le W^*$, so $\dot{W}|_{W_{\min}} \ge W_{\min}[k_{\mathrm{bio},0}(1 - W^*/K) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W}] = 0$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:aux_ep}, while $\dot{W}|_{W=K} < 0$. Hence $\Omega_1$ is jointly positively invariant. Integrating $\dot{B} = b_{\mathrm{basal}} - k_{\mathrm{clr}}B$ yields $t_0$ in Case~2. On the canonical neuronal box ($W \in [0.60, 1.25], D \in [0, 0.40], B \in [0.04, 0.50], C \in [0.65, 1.30], E_{\max} = 1.35$), $\partial\mathrm{EP}/\partial D > 0$ on $[0, 0.40]$, so $\mathrm{EP}$ attains its exact maximum at $(0.60, 0.40, 0.50, 0.65)$: $\max_{\Omega_1}\mathrm{EP} = [1.385 / (0.660 \times 1.221)] \times 0.6084 = 1.0456 < 1.150$, with $B_{\mathrm{crit}}^* = 1.2020 > 0.500$ and $B_{\mathrm{crit,mono}} = 0.3085$ (crossed at $t = 3.16\text{ d}$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}]
On $\Omega_1(D_0, B_0)$, $d_{\mathrm{ros}} \equiv 0$, $B(t) \le B_0$, and $M(t) \ge W^* - D_0$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} satisfies $\lambda_h(t) \ge \lambda_{\min}$ with
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\min} \triangleq \frac{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}K_B}{K_B + B_0} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\!\left(1 - \frac{W^* - D_0}{K}\right).
  \label{eq:lambda_min_analytic}
\end{equation}
Separating variables in $\frac{d}{dt}\ln[h/(1-h)] = -\lambda_h(t) \le -\lambda_{\min}$ and noting $1 - h(t) \ge 1 - h_0$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:prop1_bounds}. Linearizing $\dot{D}$ at $(W^*, 0, B_{\mathrm{eq}})$ where $k_{\mathrm{bio}}(1 - W^*/K) = k_{\mathrm{mito},W}$ gives $\dot{D} \approx \mu_D D$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed-Form Bottleneck Integral and Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}}
\label{app:reduction}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}]
Integrating $\frac{d}{dt}\ln[h/(1-h)] = -\lambda_h(t)$ over $[0, t]$ gives $\ln[h(t)/(1-h(t))] = \ln[h_0/(1-h_0)] - s(t)$, which inverts to Eq.~\eqref{eq:h_logistic_exact}. During Stage~1 ($u_0=1, u_1=1, u_2=0$), $k_{\mathrm{bio}}(0, N) \equiv k_{\mathrm{bio},0}$ and $B(q) = B_{\mathrm{eq}} + (B_0 - B_{\mathrm{eq}})e^{-k_{\mathrm{clr}}q}$. Let $T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}} \triangleq s^*(h_0, h_{\mathrm{gate}}) / [k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B_{\mathrm{eq}}) - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}(1 - M_0/K)]$. Integrating $V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(q))$ via $w = e^{k_{\mathrm{clr}}q}$ gives the closed-form \textit{a priori} mean competence and constant rescaling rate:
\begin{align}
  \bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}} &\triangleq \frac{1}{T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}}\int_0^{T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}} \frac{K_B\,dq}{K_B + B(q)} = \frac{K_B}{K_B + B_{\mathrm{eq}}}\!\left[1 - \frac{\ln\!\big[(K_B + B_0)/\big(K_B + B(T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}})\big)\big]}{k_{\mathrm{clr}}\,T_{1,\mathrm{apriori}}}\right], \label{eq:vlys_bar_closed} \\
  \hat{\lambda}_0 &\triangleq k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}\,\bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}} - k_{\mathrm{mito},W} - (k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}} - 1)k_{\mathrm{bio},0}\!\left(1 - \frac{M_0}{K}\right). \label{eq:lambda_hat0}
\end{align}
Subtracting Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_hat0} from Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_h} yields the integrated logit error bound
\begin{equation}
  \big|s(t) - \hat{\lambda}_0 t\big| \le \underbrace{k_{\mathrm{mito},D,\max}\!\int_0^t \big|V_{\mathrm{lys}}(B(q)) - \bar{V}_{\mathrm{lys}}\big|\,dq}_{\mathcal{E}_B(t)} + \underbrace{(k_{\mathrm{rep,adv}}-1)\frac{k_{\mathrm{bio},0}}{K}\!\int_0^t \big|M(q) - M_0\big|\,dq}_{\mathcal{E}_M(t)},
  \label{eq:multi_term_bound}
\end{equation}
and since $|d(e^\ell/[1+e^\ell])/d\ell| = h(1-h) \le 1/4$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sup_err_bound} follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence} and Actuator-Reversal Excursion Bound}
\label{app:persistence}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence}]
Any 64-frame window overlapping an $L_{\mathrm{dist}}$-frame burst contains at most $c_0 + L_{\mathrm{dist}}$ ones, which is strictly below $60$ whenever $L_{\mathrm{dist}} \le 59 - c_0$. For stationary noise, the latent Gaussian process $e_k$ is a reversible Markov chain on $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{N}(0,1))$ with $L^2$ spectral gap $\gamma_{\mathrm{spec}} = 1 - \rho$. Applying Theorem~3.3 of Paulin~\cite{paulin2015concentration} to the bounded indicator $f(e_k) = I_k \in \{0, 1\}$, multiplying by the exact conditioning factor $p_{z|Q}$, and applying Boole's union bound over $K_{\mathrm{frames}}$ steps yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:paulin_step}.
\end{proof}

Finally, during an acute post-switch insult over $[t_{\mathrm{insult}}, t_{\mathrm{insult}} + \Delta t_{\mathrm{active}}]$ with active excursion duration $\Delta t_{\mathrm{active}} \approx \delta t_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau_{\mathrm{wash}}\ln[(\mathrm{EP}_{\mathrm{peak}} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})/(\theta_{\mathrm{abort}} - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}})]$, integrating the positive ROS term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:h_dot} from $h(t_{\mathrm{insult}}) \le h_{\mathrm{gate}}$ bounds the net heteroplasmy excursion prior to actuator washout by:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta h_{\max} \le (1 - h_{\mathrm{gate}})\,k_{\mathrm{dmg}}\int_{t_{\mathrm{insult}}}^{t_{\mathrm{insult}} + \Delta t_{\mathrm{active}}} \max\!\big(0,\, \mathrm{EP}(q) - \theta_{\mathrm{GSH}}\big)\,dq.
  \label{eq:rollback_excursion_bound}
\end{equation}
