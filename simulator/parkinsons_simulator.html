The Mitophagy Survival Paradox: How to Purge 58% Damaged Mitochondria Without Starving or Poisoning the Neuron

In Substantia Nigra pars compacta (SNc) pacemaker neurons, baseline mutant/damaged mitochondrial DNA (D) reaches 58%–68%, leaving only 32%–42% healthy wild-type mitochondria (W). This creates a critical clinical dilemma: if you stimulate biogenesis on Day 1, you clone the damaged mitochondria; but if you aggressively purge the 58% damaged mitochondria all at once, total cellular ATP drops below the Pacemaker Survival Floor (ATP < 0.45×) and kills the neuron.

1. What Removes Only Damaged Mitochondria?
Voltage-Selective PINK1/Parkin Mitophagy (PDB 6EQI)

Healthy W mitochondria maintain a high inner-membrane voltage (ΔΨm ≈ -160 mV), which imports and degrades the sensor kinase PINK1 so healthy mitochondria are spared. Damaged D mitochondria lose voltage; PINK1 accumulates on their outer surface and tags only broken mitochondria with Ser65-phospho-ubiquitin for engulfment by autophagosomes (boosted in Stage 1 by Kinetin Riboside + Urolithin A).

2. How Can Purging Kill the Neuron (3 Failure Modes)?
Starvation Abyss, Lysosomal Rupture & O₂ Burn

Unstaged mitophagy kills neurons in three ways: (a) Starvation: deleting 58% of mitochondrial mass without metabolic support drops ATP below the 0.45× Na⁺/K⁺ pump survival floor; (b) Lysosomal Pileup: digested mitochondria jam crippled GBA1 lysosomes (PDB 2NSX); (c) Hyperoxia Burn: fewer mitochondria consume less O₂, spiking tissue pO₂ = 1.63× and oxidizing dopamine into toxic DA-o-Quinone.

3. How Does PD-BCR Keep the Neuron Alive During the Purge?
Stage 0 Shield + Stage 1 Ketone Bridge + Day-27 Brake

(1) Stage 0 (Days 1–14): Ambroxol unclogs GBA1 lysosomes and GBT601+PT2399 clamps excess O₂ before purging starts. (2) Stage 1 (Days 15–27): C8-MCT Ketones (β-OHB) supercharge ATP output in the remaining 42% healthy W pool so ATP stays >0.75×. (3) Day-27 GDF15 Gate: Brakes mitophagy as soon as h < 8% and switches to Stage 2 W cloning.
